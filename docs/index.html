The Passiones of St Kilian, and the record of how they were transmitted

A critical dossier of the two medieval Passiones of Kilian, Colonatus and Totnan — the Passio minor (BHL 4660) and the Passio maior (BHL 4661) — the history of the Würzburg cult, and the Killion–Kouns family archive that grew up around them.

Assembled by Nicholas Shane Kouns. The paradigm this work sits beside carries his mother's name, Anna Rose Killion Kouns (1931–2015), and so does this archive.

The surname

Polygenetic. Irish Killian and Killion descend from Ó Cillín / Ó Cilleáin, a diminutive of Ceallach; German Kilian from the saint's name used as a baptismal name, which spread through Franconia because of the Würzburg cult. Two unrelated roots, one spelling. Bearers of the name were never one kin group and were never expected to be — which is how a chain of the name crosses fourteen centuries without a chain of bodies.

What this collection asserts

That two Latin hagiographic works survive concerning Kilian, Colonatus and Totnan, and that their text, their manuscripts, their printed editions and their modern scholarship can be recorded accurately.

That an Irish or Irish-associated missionary remembered as Kilian, with two companions, was active near Würzburg before the diocese was organised, came into conflict with the ducal household, and died violently. That Burchard, the first bishop of Würzburg, translated relics venerated as theirs, traditionally in 752.

That the Augsburg Kilian family were real goldsmiths and engravers, documented in civic, guild and museum records from the sixteenth century. That the Cuntze / Kuntze / Kouns family were real Siegerland ironworkers who entered Virginia with the Germanna migration of 1714. That the two lines meet in twentieth-century Kentucky.

What it does not assert

Boundary

No descent from Saint Kilian. No hagiographic source gives Kilian a wife, a child, a sibling line or a hereditary estate. He is presented throughout as a celibate cleric among clerical companions. The shared name — Killena, Chilianus, Kilian, Killian, Killion — is onomastic and devotional continuity, not biological continuity.

No eyewitness authority for the seventh century. Neither Passio is a contemporary record. The minor postdates the events by at least decades and probably by a century; the maior by roughly three.

The reading text is not a critical edition and has not been collated against Levison. It says so itself, and the textual notes mark four defects that must be repaired against the standard edition.

No mathematical claim. The Kouns–Killion Paradigm is a separate body of work in separate repositories. Where it appears here it is confined to interpretive/ and labelled as interpretation, never as a finding of the medieval sources.

These boundaries are checked on every push by a script that fails the build if any of them stops being stated.
