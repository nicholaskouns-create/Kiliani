The Passiones of St Kilian, and the record of how they were transmitted

A critical dossier of the two medieval Passiones of Kilian, Colonatus and Totnan — the Passio minor (BHL 4660) and the Passio maior (BHL 4661) — the history of their transmission, and the Killion–Kouns family archive that grew up around them.

Assembled by Nicholas Shane Kouns. The paradigm this work sits beside carries his mother's name, Anna Rose Killion Kouns (1931–2015), and so does this archive.

What this collection asserts

That two Latin hagiographic works survive concerning Kilian, Colonatus and Totnan, and that their text, their manuscripts, their printed editions and their modern scholarship can be recorded accurately.

That an Irish or Irish-associated missionary remembered as Kilian, with two companions, was active near Würzburg before the diocese was organised, came into conflict with the ducal household, and died violently. That Burchard, the first bishop of Würzburg, translated relics venerated as theirs, traditionally in 752.

What it does not assert

Boundary

No descent from Saint Kilian. No hagiographic source gives Kilian a wife, a child, a sibling line or a hereditary estate. He is presented throughout as a celibate cleric among clerical companions. The shared name — Killena, Chilianus, Kilian, Killian, Killion — is onomastic and devotional continuity, not biological continuity.

The reading text is not a critical edition and has not been collated against Levison. It says so itself, and the textual notes mark four defects that must be repaired against the standard edition.

These boundaries are checked on every push by a script that fails the build if any of them stops being stated.

Open data

Everything behind the apparatus browser is in the repository and downloadable directly. The hand transcription is the primary datum; everything else regenerates from it.
