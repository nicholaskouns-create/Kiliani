# Kilian (Chillien) of Aubigny

**d. c. 670 · feast 13 November · Aubigny-en-Artois, near Arras**

A different man from the martyr of Würzburg, and the one most likely to be
mistaken for him.

---

## The sources

The *Dictionary of Irish Biography* gives him as: **[B]**

> Kilian (Cilian, Cillianus, Chillenus) (d. 670) of Aubigny (near Arras,
> northern France) was a hermit-saint and evangeliser.

He appears **inside the Lives of Fiacre and Faro** rather than in a Life of his
own. The narrative: returning from a pilgrimage to Rome, he visited his kinsman
or acquaintance **Fiacre** in Brie. **Faro, bishop of Meaux**, sent him to
evangelise Artois. A nobleman named **Eulfus** gave him a site at Aubigny after
the **miracle of the mended glass chalice**.

### A caution on the "Life"

The DIB states that Kilian's Life was written by **Hildegaire, bishop of Meaux,
in 869**, preserved in two Douai manuscripts of the tenth and twelfth centuries.

Hildegar is the author of the ***Vita Faronis***. So what the Douai manuscripts
most likely preserve is **the Kilian episode within that work**, not an
independent vita. Treat the attribution of a separate Life with caution until the
Douai manuscripts are examined.

## Relics

At the church he founded at **Aubigny-en-Artois**. **[C]**

## Later accretions

Two claims circulate that are hagiographic growth rather than early evidence
**[D]**:

- that Colgan's manuscript styles him a **bishop**
- that he was **offered the papacy**

The second is the kind of detail that attaches itself to a saint centuries after
the fact and should carry no weight.

## The conflation risk — stated precisely

The *Passio minor* of the Würzburg Kilian says of its subject:

> *Et tunc iam alio vocabatur nomine **Chilianus***

"And by then he was called by another name, Chilianus."

**Chillenus / Chillien is the Aubigny saint's name.** The two saints therefore
share a Latin name-form in the primary sources, not merely in modern indexes.

What is known:

- **No conflation has been identified in the medieval manuscript tradition or in
  the liturgy.** The research behind this file found none.
- **Popular saint websites do file them under shared name-tags**, which is how a
  modern reader is most likely to merge them.

The question of whether any medieval witness confused them is **open**. It would
be answered by checking whether any Kilian of Würzburg manuscript carries
Aubigny material or a 13 November date, or vice versa.

## Why this saint belongs in this archive

Because a second seventh-century Irish Kilian, remembered in France, returning
from Rome, with a name form the Würzburg *Passio* explicitly assigns to its own
subject, is exactly the sort of figure a devotional genealogy will reach for and
exactly the sort a critical one must separate out.

He is also, independently, worth knowing about.
