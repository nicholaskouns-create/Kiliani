# The saints named Kilian

**There is more than one.** At least three individuals bearing this name have a
documented cult, and they are routinely confused — including on pages consulted
by this archive.

| Saint | Died | Feast | Place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kilian of Würzburg** | trad. 689 | **8 July** | Würzburg, Franconia |
| **Kilian (Chillien) of Aubigny** | c. 670 | **13 November** | Aubigny-en-Artois, near Arras |
| **Cillíne Droichtech** | 752 | **3 July** | Iona |

All three are Irish or remembered as Irish. Two died in the same decade. Two were
returning from, or associated with, pilgrimage to Rome. The confusion is
understandable, and the archive's own earlier material did not distinguish them
at all.

## Why this matters here

The *Passio minor* says that Killena "was called by another name, **Chilianus**"
— and *Chillien* is precisely the name of the Aubigny saint. The two saints
therefore share a Latin name-form in the sources themselves.

No conflation has been found **in the medieval manuscripts or in the liturgy**.
That question is open rather than settled. But popular saint sites do file them
under shared name-tags, and any claim about "St Kilian" taken from a devotional
website should be checked against which Kilian is meant.

## And one who is not a saint at all

**"St Clegwallabog Killian (1490–1555)"**, which appears as generation 2 on this
family's lineage plates, corresponds to no saint in any authoritative source.
