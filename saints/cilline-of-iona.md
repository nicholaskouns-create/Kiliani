# Cillíne of Iona, and the Ó Cillín clerics

---

## Two abbots of Iona

| Abbot | Office | Cult |
|---|---|---|
| **Cilléne Fota** ("the Tall") | abbot 724–726 | no evidence of cult found |
| **Cilléne Droichtech** ("the Bridge-maker") | abbot 726–752 | **commemorated 3 July** in the *Martyrology of Donegal* |

**[B, annalistic lists]** · the Donegal commemoration **[A, via secondary
quotation]**

Only **Droichtech** has evidence of a cult. The epithet *Droichtech*,
bridge-maker, is a good one for an archive about continuity to have stumbled on,
though nothing follows from that.

Note the dates: Droichtech was abbot of Iona **until 752** — the very year
traditionally given for Burchard's translation of the Würzburg relics. A
coincidence, and a reminder of how dense the Irish-Continental world of that
century was.

## The Iona tradition in the Kilian legend

St Kilian's Heritage Centre at Mullagh records a claim that the Würzburg Kilian
spent time as a monk **on Iona**. That claim has **no early basis**. It traces
back to **Trithemius**, the late-fifteenth- and early-sixteenth-century abbot
whose historical writing is notoriously inventive. **[D]**

The presence of two Iona abbots named Cillíne in exactly the relevant period is a
plausible route by which such a claim could have formed — but that is a
hypothesis about the legend's growth, not evidence for it.

## Churchmen who are not saints

The Irish annals carry several Ó Cillín clerics. They are **clerics, not
saints**, and should not be added to any list of saints of the name:

| Name | Died | Office |
|---|---|---|
| Cormac ua Cillín | 966 | *comarba* of Tuamgraney |
| Conall ua Cillín | 1026 | — |
| Cormac ua Cillín | 1106 | of Clonmacnoise |

These men bear the surname from which Irish Killian and Killion descend — see
[../family/surname.md](../family/surname.md) — which makes them relevant to the
name's history and irrelevant to its sanctity.

## How many saints of the name?

**At least three** have a documented cult: Würzburg (8 July), Aubigny
(13 November), Iona/Droichtech (3 July).

The Irish martyrologies may hold further minor Cillíne commemorations that the
research behind this file could not enumerate. The count is a floor, not a
ceiling.
