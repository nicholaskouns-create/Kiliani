# Sancti Kiliani

A critical dossier of the two medieval *Passiones* of St Kilian of Würzburg, the
history of their transmission, and the Killion–Kouns family archive that grew up
around them.

Assembled by Nicholas Shane Kouns. The paradigm this work sits beside carries his
mother's name, Anna Rose Killion Kouns (1931–2015), and so does this archive.

---

## What this collection asserts

That two Latin hagiographic works survive concerning Kilian, Colonatus and
Totnan — the *Passio minor* (BHL 4660) and the *Passio maior* (BHL 4661) — and
that their text, their manuscripts, their printed editions and their modern
scholarship can be recorded accurately.

That an Irish or Irish-associated missionary remembered as Kilian, with two
companions, was active near Würzburg before the diocese was organised, came into
conflict with the ducal household, and died violently. That Burchard, the first
bishop of Würzburg, translated relics venerated as theirs, traditionally in 752.
That the cult was established in the Carolingian period and became the
institutional self-understanding of the see.

That the Augsburg Kilian family were real goldsmiths and engravers, documented in
civic, guild and museum records from the sixteenth century. That the Cuntze /
Kuntze / Kouns family were real Siegerland ironworkers who entered Virginia with
the Germanna migration of 1714. That the two lines meet in twentieth-century
Kentucky.

## What this collection does not assert

**No descent from Saint Kilian.** No hagiographic source gives Kilian a wife, a
child, a sibling line or a hereditary estate. He is presented throughout as a
celibate cleric among clerical companions. The shared name — Killena, Chilianus,
Kilian, Killian, Killion — is onomastic and devotional continuity, not biological
continuity. An unbroken maternal line to the seventh century cannot be
established from any source known to this archive, and nothing here should be
read as establishing one.

**No eyewitness authority for the seventh century.** Neither *Passio* is a
contemporary record. The *minor* postdates the events by at least decades and
probably by a century; the *maior* by roughly three.

**No papal canonisation act.** No authentic medieval canonisation bull for Kilian
has been located, and none should be expected: his cult predates centralised
papal canonisation.

**No mathematical claim.** The Kouns–Killion Paradigm is a separate body of work
and lives in separate repositories. Where it appears here it is confined to
[`interpretive/`](interpretive/) and labelled as interpretation, never as a
finding of the medieval sources.

## How the collection is arranged

The arrangement is the argument. Each directory is a different evidence class,
and nothing crosses the walls between them without saying so.

| Directory | Holds | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| [`texts/`](texts/) | The Latin texts and facing translations, with apparatus, witnesses and editions | Medieval sources and working editions of them |
| [`recensions/`](recensions/) | The history of this archive's own editions — what was printed, what was wrong, what was corrected | Editorial history |
| [`scholarship/`](scholarship/) | Research reports, the ecclesiastical review, bibliography | Secondary literature |
| [`family/`](family/) | The maternal Killion and paternal Kouns lines, graded by confidence; the Augsburg artistic corpus | Genealogical record, mixed confidence, explicitly labelled |
| [`interpretive/`](interpretive/) | The KKP-R layer | Interpretation, not historical claim |

[`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) gives the editorial status of every file in one table.

## The texts

- **[*Passio minor*](texts/passio-minor/)** — BHL 4660. The earlier continuous
  narrative. Latin and English in parallel, with eight textual notes, a witness
  list, an edition list and an open verification queue.
- **[*Passio maior*](texts/passio-maior/)** — BHL 4661. The tenth-century
  expansion. The bilingual text in this archive is **a modern conflation, not the
  medieval work**; the reason is set out in
  [CONFLATION-NOTICE.md](texts/passio-maior/CONFLATION-NOTICE.md) and the file is
  labelled accordingly.

Cite the *Passio minor* from Levison, *MGH Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum* 5
(Hannover, 1910). Nothing in this repository is a substitute for that edition.

## Open questions

This archive keeps its unresolved problems in the open rather than tidying them
away. The standing ones:

- The dating of the *Passio minor*: c. 752 or c. 840 or the repertory's 750–850.
  See [texts/passio-minor/README.md](texts/passio-minor/README.md).
- Whether Vienna ÖNB 552 or St Gallen 566 is the earliest witness. The sources in
  this archive disagree with each other. See
  [texts/passio-minor/witnesses.md](texts/passio-minor/witnesses.md).
- "St Clegwallabog Killian" (1490–1555), which no authority corroborates.
- Whether "Hans Kilian (1516–1583)" is an Augsburg progenitor or a conflation
  with the Neuburg/Passau printer.
- George W. Kouns (1863–1939), where same-name confusion is already visible.

These are listed with their next archival steps in
[family/open-questions.md](family/open-questions.md) and
[texts/passio-minor/verification-queue.md](texts/passio-minor/verification-queue.md).

## Conventions

⟨angle brackets⟩ enclose editorial supplements. […] marks a lacuna. Italics in
the English mark words with no Latin equivalent. Numbered notes in the
translations refer to [the apparatus](texts/passio-minor/apparatus.md).

Three confidence labels are used throughout the family material, and they mean
the same thing everywhere:

- **documented** — anchored in a primary record or an authoritative museum,
  library or biographical reference
- **likely** — consistent derivative reconstruction, underlying record not yet
  inspected
- **speculative** — uncorroborated, or in tension with stronger evidence
