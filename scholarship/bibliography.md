## Doctoral dissertation

**Joanna Thornborough**, *The "Passiones" of St. Kilian: Cult, Politics and
Society in the Carolingian and Ottonian Worlds*. PhD thesis, University of St
Andrews, 2015. Supervised by James T. Palmer; funded by the Arts and Humanities
Research Council.

The strongest single modern synthesis centred on the Kilian dossier. Covers
c. 700–1000, comparing the texts, the relic cult, geographical diffusion,
political context and the construction of Würzburg's institutional memory. Its
central methodological contribution is to treat saints' lives as **arguments
rather than transparent chronicles**, in the line of Ian Wood and James Palmer
and drawing on Julia Smith, Thomas Head and Raymond Van Dam.

A multilingual repository and catalogue search across English, German, French,
Italian and Latin located **no second independently catalogued doctorate**
devoted specifically to the Kilian *Passiones*. That does not prove none exists;
it establishes that Thornborough's is the identifiable, repository-deposited
dedicated doctorate in the accessible international corpus.

**Two corrections to claims made elsewhere in this archive:**

- Karl Firsching, *Die deutschen Bearbeitungen der Kilianslegende unter
  besonderer Berücksichtigung deutscher Legendarhandschriften des Mittelalters*
  (1973), is an important monograph on vernacular transmission. The catalogue
  records reviewed **do not establish it as a Kilian-focused doctoral
  dissertation**.
- Emmerich's 1896 book was a **historical-critical monograph, not a
  dissertation**. His honorary theological doctorate the same year was not earned
  through the Kilian study.

## Holy See material

**Benedict XVI**, homily at Erfurt, **24 September 2011**. Official Vatican text
in German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Polish and Portuguese. All seven
versions carry the same substantive assertion:

> Before the Anglo-Saxon missionaries, Saint Kilian, an itinerant missionary from
> Ireland, was active in Thuringia. Together with two companions, he died a
> martyr at Würzburg because he had criticised the moral misconduct of the Duke
> of Thuringia who resided there.

The homily **receives the devotional core** of the tradition. It does **not**
pronounce on the authorship or dating of either *Passio*, the historicity of the
Roman journey, the date 689, or the guilt of Geilana rather than Gozbert.

**No authentic medieval papal canonisation bull for Kilian has been located**,
and none should be expected. **Formal papal canonisation begins only with Ulrich
of Augsburg in 993** — some two and a half centuries after Kilian's cult was
already established. Claims that a named pope canonised Kilian on a precise date
should not be used without production of an authentic act, register entry or
diplomatic edition.

⚠ **Correction to an earlier state of this bibliography.** The sentence "Before
the Anglo-Saxon missionaries, Saint Kilian, an itinerant missionary from
Ireland…" was attributed here to the official vatican.va text in seven
languages. It belongs to the **provisional text released through Vatican Radio**.
The official text names Kilian among the Erfurt patrons but does not carry that
sentence. See [../cult/worldwide.md](../cult/worldwide.md).

For the distinction between a manuscript held in the Vatican Library and a
document issued by the Holy See, see
