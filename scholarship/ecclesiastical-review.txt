ABSTRACT
This review examines a recent private research edition of the Passio minor Kiliani, the earliest hagiographic
stratum of St. Kilian of Würzburg (ca. 640-689), reconstructed from Franz Emmerich, Der heilige Kilian
(1896) and its base manuscripts St. Gallen 566, Vienna 552, and Munich 4585. The edition, prepared by
Nicholas Shane Kouns within a larger archival project linking manuscript transmission, relic translation,
diocesan formation, Augsburg print culture, and a documented American family archive, offers a bilingual
Latin-English text with sevenfold structural analysis and a reproducible digital provenance architecture. The
present recension evaluates philological accuracy, translational choices, historical-ecclesiastical
contextualization, and the integration of devotional genealogy with critical method. It situates the work
within current scholarship, notably Joanna Thornborough's thesis on the Passiones in Carolingian and
Ottonian worlds (St Andrews, 2015), the MGH and Acta Sanctorum tradition, and contemporary Würzburg
diocesan historiography. It concludes that the edition constitutes a citable working text of pastoral and
scholarly utility, provided the interpretive mathematical layer (Ω_c = 47/125) is maintained as a separately
labeled analytical appendix, as the author himself proposes.

Keywords: Passio minor Kiliani, St. Kilian, Hagiography, Würzburg, Emmerich, MGH, BHL 4660, Manuscript
Transmission, Translatio, Digital Humanities



1. Introduction: Corpus and Intent
The dossier under review comprises three public-facing objects: (i) a one-page infographic summary of the
Passio minor Kiliani with seven narrative stations; (ii) a two-page bilingual source plate with apparatus
notes; and (iii) a nineteen-page monograph, From Passio minor Kiliani to the Modern Archive (2026),
integrating five deliverables. The author explicitly distinguishes documentary history from interpretive
mathematics, while cross-referencing them through explicit provenance. This methodological
self-awareness is rare in private family hagiography projects and merits serious attention.

The titular Kouns Family Tree — Recursive Identity Source Plate and Maternal Line Attractor Node
language belongs to the author's larger Kouns-Killion Philosophical-Recursive (KKP-R) project. For
ecclesiastical review, that layer must be evaluated as interpretive gloss, not as textual claim within the
Passio itself. The author correctly proposes this separation in Chapter 7 of his monograph.


2. Status Quaestionis of the Kiliani Corpus
The Kiliani corpus comprises two early medieval families: Passio minor (BHL 4660), short, austere, dated
by most scholars to ca. 730-752, shortly after Bishop Burchard's translatio of 752; and Passio maior / Vita
longior (BHL 4661-4664), amplified with prologue, Atalongus miracle, and institutional foundation
narrative, dated 9th-10th century. Emmerich (1896) remains the standard critical collation, printing the
minor on pp. 3-10 from St. Gallen, Stiftsbibliothek Cod. 566 (s. IX ex./X in.), Vienna, Österreichische
Nationalbibliothek Cod. 552 (s. IX med.), Munich, BSB Clm 4585 (s. IX-XI), collated in Acta Sanctorum
Julii II and by Krusch-Levison, MGH Passiones.

Modern scholarship has moved from positivist fact-extraction — e.g., the older discussion whether Kilian
was raised to the purple before leaving his native land versus at Rome — to functional reading.
Thornborough (2015) frames the Passiones as 'textual arguments' in the lineage of Ian Wood and James
Palmer, operating within cultic and political contexts, combining cultural history approaches of Julia Smith,
Thomas Head, and Raymond Van Dam. She notes that Kilian is presented in the Passio minor as a holy
man in a distinctly Bonifatian mould, and that the text insists on proper licence and authority before
mission. This is crucial for current missiology.


3. Manuscript Basis and Editorial Method
The Kouns edition faithfully follows Emmerich's base. The apparatus notes: Critical base: St. Gallen,
Stiftsbibliothek, Cod. 566; Vienna, ÖNB, Cod. 552; Munich, BSB, Clm 4585; and related MSS collated in
Acta Sanctorum Julii II and Krusch-Levison, MGH Passiones. Name variants are noted: Killena / Kyllena /
Chilianus / Kilianus / Kylianus; companion names fluid: Colonatus / Colmanus; occasional additional
figures in minor versions. This matches the manuscript reality. For publication grade, desiderata are: (a)
siglum table with foliation, (b) IIIF-compatible image citations, (c) explicit distinction between Emmerich's
emendations and manuscript readings, (d) line numbering.

Editorial boundary is clearly stated: the edition is a critical working edition rather than final diplomatic
transcription of every witness, with conceptual stemma to be replaced by witness-by-witness codicological
collation in final edition. This transparency is appropriate for Edition 1.0.


4. Philological Analysis: Latin Text and English Translation
Incipit: Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine, quem Scottica tellus de magno edidit genere. The
translation 'There was a man of venerable life named Killena, whom the Scottish land brought forth from a
great family' is accurate. Note: Scottica tellus in 8th-century Frankish usage denotes Ireland (Hibernia), not
modern Scotland; a gloss is advisable for pastoral use. Qui etiam a puerili aetate magnum habuit studium
sacras discere litteras — 'From boyhood he also had great zeal to learn the sacred letters' — correctly
renders litterae sacrae as Christian letters, not classical literature. exinde pontificale didicit regere culmen
— 'from there he learned to rule the pontifical height' — literal and correct; culmen is office, dignity.
quicquid ad animarum lucrum, quicquid ad propagationem fidei pertinere cognoscebat — double
missionary motive characteristic of early medieval missionary hagiography.

Vocatio et Discessus: The Vulgate citation (Luc. IX, 23) is correctly retained: Qui vult venire post me,
abneget semet ipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me. nudos sequi Christum — translated 'to follow
Christ naked' — correctly preserves technical apostolic poverty (nudus = stripped of possessions). firmiter
coadunati, relictis omnibus, de propria perrexerunt patria — 'firmly united, having left all things, set out'
— good rendering of inchoative perfect. Companion list: Colonato ac Gallone et Arnuvale presbyteris et
Totnano diacono, aliis septem illis adiunctis — translation 'with seven others joined to them' is standard.

Iter Romanum: Sed illic venientes et apostolicum virum Johannem non invenientes, quoniam iam
defunctus est, a sancto papa Conone... susceptus est — 'But coming there and not finding the apostolic man
John, since he was already dead, was received amiably and honorably by the holy Pope Conon' — captures
amabiliter et honorifice pairing. The Pope Conon (686-687) chronological datum is retained without
harmonization, as critical method requires.

Missio et Conversio, Conflictus, Martyrium, Translatio: Audiens autem dux Gozbertus verbum
praedicationis eius, cum omni populo suo credidit et baptizatus est — concise baptismal formula. quae
fuerat uxor fratris eius, quia hoc christianae religioni contrarium esse cognoscebat — illicit marriage to
brother's widow, Lev 18:16 typology. corrupta suis illecebris — 'corrupted by her allurements' — apt for
illecebrae. in stabulo equorum occiderent et corpora eorum ibi sepelirent cum vasis sacris et vestimentis et
scripturis sanctis — 'in the horse stable... with sacred vessels and vestments and holy writings' — exact,
with important inventio motif: concealed martyrdom with liturgical apparatus for later discovery.

Postea vero, cum Burchardus episcopus Wirziburgensis, praecepto et consilio Zachariae papae et Pippini
regis, corpora sanctorum martyrum cum honore transtulisset et in ecclesia beatae Mariae in monte
collocasset, miraculis et sanitatibus claruerunt. Translation 'by the precept and counsel of Pope Zacharias
and King Pippin... they became renowned through miracles and healings' correctly renders sanitatibus as
healings. This translatio of 752 is the ecclesiastical foundation charter of Würzburg, linking martyrdom to
episcopal institution.


5. Hagiographical Theology: Sevenfold Structure
The author's seven titles — Incipit, Vocatio et Discessus, Iter Romanum, Missio et Conversio, Conflictus de
Matrimonio, Martyrium, Translatio — are not in the manuscripts but constitute a sound
liturgical-hagiographical hermeneutic: origin, call and exodus, Roman authorization, proclamation and
conversion, prophetic confrontation, martyrial witness, and translation/ glorification. This mirrors the
classical triad of vita - passio - translatio expanded for missionary hagiography. Each station has biblical
typology: Luke 9:23, Abrahamic departure, Petrine authorization, Acts conversion, John the
Baptist-Herodias parallel for Gozbert/Geilana, and proto-martyr Stephen.


6. Historical-Ecclesiastical Context
The Passio minor must be read east of the Rhine, in a region without strong Christian-Roman continuity.
Thornborough: Thuringia produced new saints and cults in this period, yet they all operated within the
overarching framework of the well-established religious phenomenon of saints' cults. The insistence on
Roman licence reflects the Bonifatian reform: no mission without licentia. The later rewriting by Stephan of
Novara around 950 — After arriving at the bishopric around 950, the Italian scholar Stephan of Novara
rewrote the story of St. Kilian for a contemporary audience... to promote its saint and reassert the
bishopric's position in the burgeoning Ottonian Reich — shows the Passio's political function. The Kouns
edition's focus on the earliest layer therefore serves current scholarship's interest in Carolingian memory
and identity.

Liturgically, Kilian remains patron of the Diocese of Würzburg, feast July 8, enshrined in Neumünster over
the traditional martyrdom site, with his reputed New Testament preserved until 1803 in the cathedral
treasury. Contemporary diocesan literature presents Kilian as model of peregrinatio and new evangelization
— directly relevant to synodal discussions of missionary discipleship.


7. Genealogical and Archival Dimensions
The monograph integrates Deliverable II: a canonical maternal Kilian/Killion lineage from St. Kilian
(hagiographic layer) through Hans Kilian (1516-1583), Bartholomaeus I, Wolfgang (1581-1662), Philipp
(1628-1693), Johannes Jacob Balthasar (1689-1742), Johann/Andreas Killion (1732-1781), William
(1768-1850), John J. (1813-1890), Arthur (1848-1920), Alexander (1885-1961), Anna Rose Killion Kouns
(1931-2015) to Nicholas Shane Kouns (1965-). The documentary layers are properly distinguished:
hagiographic and ecclesiastical for the 7th century, civic and guild records for Augsburg, museum and print
catalogues for engravers, parish, land, census, and family records for American centuries. For critical
genealogy, an unbroken maternal line to ca. 640 cannot be established from extant sources before ca. 1200.
As the author frames it as canonical for this research package based on LDS genealogical reconstruction
and family archive, it is best presented as traditio familiae and devotional lineage motivating scholarship,
not replacing documentary method. This distinction is maintained in the edition.

Deliverable III, catalogue of Augsburg Kilian artistic works — Lucas Kilian Holy Family (Met
51.501.6485), Wolfgang Kilian Fuggerorum Imagines (1620), Large Map of Augsburg (1626), Discovery
of New World copper plate (Smithsonian nmah_819205), Philipp Kilian illustrations, etc. — is reception
history, not part of the medieval cult, but valuable for Counter-Reformation print culture. Deliverable IV,
church continuum, correctly sequences martyrdom (689), translation (ca. 752), circulation (9th-10th),
liturgy and visual culture, American parish continuity, modern archive.

9. Critical Appraisal and Recommendations
Strengths: (1) Clear choice of earliest recension as base; (2) Accurate, readable translation preserving
technical hagiographic diction; (3) Explicit apparatus of name variants and fluid companion tradition; (4)
Sevenfold theological structure useful for lectio and catechesis; (5) Integration of manuscript, institutional,
artistic, and family memory into one auditable archive; (6) Methodological honesty about editorial
boundary.

Desiderata for publication grade: (a) Add witness-level foliation and repository shelfmark verification; (b)
Include BHL 4660 incipit and explicit citations; (c) Provide full collation table of Emmerich variants; (d)
Add commentary on Merovingian Latin features and biblical allusions; (e) For ecclesiastical review,
include liturgical use note and German translation comparison; (f) Maintain strict separation of
documentary layers and interpretive mathematics in final PDF rendering; (g) Secure image rights for St
Gallen, Vienna, Munich plates.


10. Conclusion
The Kouns working edition of Passio minor Kiliani constitutes a citable, bilingual, structurally annotated
text of pastoral and scholarly utility. It restores attention to the earliest, most restrained layer of the Kilian
tradition — the Bonifatian model of authorized mission, prophetic confrontation with power in the
Gozbert-Geilana episode, humble martyrdom in the horse stable, and episcopal translation by Burchard
under Zacharias and Pippin. Situated within current scholarship on cult, politics, and society in Carolingian
and Ottonian worlds, and linked to a reproducible digital archive, it merits inclusion in diocesan and
academic Kiliani studies. Its strongest contribution for a Vatican-facing application is demonstration of a
bounded corpus, existing bilingual editions and visual plates, direct research relationship to Würzburg and
medieval hagiography, and a credible digital humanities plan, motivated but not replaced by personal
genealogical stake.
