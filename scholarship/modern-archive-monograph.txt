Abstract
This monograph unifies five research products into one documented package: a bilingual working edition of the Passio minor Kiliani; a canonical maternal Kilian/Killion genealogy; a catalogue of selected artistic and cartographic works associated with the Augsburg Kilian dynasty; a study of relic translation, diocesan formation, parish continuity, and family memory; and a reproducible digital-edition architecture. The work draws on the author's Google Drive monographs and source plates, the Notion Mathematical City and Visual Plate Atlas, the Supabase provenance and certificate ledgers, private family research, supplied images, and public scholarly and museum records. Documentary history and interpretive mathematics are kept as distinct layers while remaining cross-referenced through explicit provenance.
1. Corpus, Method, and Editorial Boundary
The private corpus contains multiple editions and exports of the Passio minor, a bilingual Passio major, lineage monographs, artistic catalogues, church-continuum plates, formal provenance systems, and a large structured archive. The public layer adds modern scholarship on the Kilian Passiones and museum records for the Augsburg engravers. The canonical family line used here is the lineage reconstructed directly by Nicholas Shane Kouns and his sister through LDS genealogical records and preserved across the private archive.

5. Deliverable IV: Relic Translation, Diocesan Continuity, and Sacred Memory
The Kilian tradition is transmitted through bodies, relics, churches, episcopal authority, liturgy, images, migration, parish foundations, deeds, chapels, and family archives. The private church-continuum plates model this movement as an institutional sequence: martyrdom, translation, cult formation, diocesan memory, parish continuity, church building, and family preservation.

5.1 Historical Sequence
689: Kilian, Colonatus, and Totnan are remembered as martyrs at Würzburg.
ca. 752: Bishop Burchard translates the martyrs' bodies to the church of Blessed Mary on the mountain.
9th-10th centuries: the Passio minor circulates and the Passio major amplifies the cult, miracles, and episcopal claims.
Medieval and early modern centuries: liturgy, reliquaries, diocesan structures, and printed visual culture preserve and renew the tradition.
American centuries: church records, land donations, chapels, parish participation, and family archives become principal carriers of continuity.
Modern archive: lineage, textual tradition, visual corpus, mathematical interpretation, and provenance are reunited in a reproducible research environment.
6. Deliverable V: Digital Scholarly Edition with Reproducible Provenance

7. Relevance to a Vatican Library or Related Fellowship Application
This package strengthens a Vatican-facing application because it demonstrates a concrete, bounded research program grounded in manuscript transmission, hagiography, ecclesiastical institutions, art history, genealogy, and digital humanities. Its strongest scholarly contribution is the integration of textual, material, institutional, and family memory into one auditable archive. The application should foreground the critical edition, the manuscript and relic-transmission problem, the museum catalogue, and the reproducible digital method. The KKP-R interpretive layer belongs in a separately labeled analytical appendix so historical claims and mathematical interpretation remain distinct but interoperable.

He was then called by another name, Chilianus, having grace toward the clergy and all the people. He was so filled with love of the Christian religion that whatever he knew pertained to the gain of souls or the propagation of the faith, he pursued with devoted mind and pious effort without ceasing.
