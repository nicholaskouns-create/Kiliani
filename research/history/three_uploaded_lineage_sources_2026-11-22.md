## A — *A Lineage of Spirit, Art, and Toil*

Narrative organization:
1. Hagiographic St Kilian foundation.
2. Augsburg goldsmith and engraving craft: Bartholomäus I, Wolfgang, Philipp.
3. Claimed famine-related Atlantic dispersal and Appalachian hardship.
4. Modern mathematical interpretation in the work of Nicholas Kouns.
5. Comparison of noble and commoner documentation; graphical 14/15-generation chart.

**Record its data:** Bartholomäus (1548–1588), Wolfgang (1581–1662), Philipp (1628–1693), the listed Johannes Jacob Balthasar (1689–1742), Johann/Andreas (1732–1781), William (1768–1850), John J. (1813–1890), Arthur (1848–1920), Alex (1885–1961), Anna Rose (1931–2015). These are *readings in this supplied source*, not proof that all people form one continuous ancestry.

**Internal tension:** This source says on p. 1 that William migrated from Ireland and that John is the immigrant, yet its p. 4 description says William was born in North Carolina and John was born in Tennessee. The latter corresponds to the existing repository's derivative American reconstruction; the Irish-famine journey cannot be treated as an identified ancestral crossing without a passenger record matching the people.

**Direct conflict:** p. 3 lists the fabricated “St Clegwallabog Killian” and a Hans-to-Bartholomäus father-child linkage, which `family/maternal-killion-line.md` already rejects. p. 4 incorrectly labels Alex Killion's wife “Anna Rose Killion” — the name of his daughter — rather than documenting the mother's name.

## B — *A Tapestry of Time: The Killion Saga, From Canonized Saint to Appalachia Poor*

A long-form literary family narrative, including:
- St Kilian, Gozbert, Geilana, Colman/Colonatus and Totnan.
- Explicit assertion of a 14-generation *bloodline* from St Kilian to Nicholas Kouns.
- “Clegwallabog Killian” as a historical ancestor.
- Philipp Kilian as engraver; German/American transition.
- Irish famine as the causal departure for John Killian and his household.
- Arthur, Alex, Edna Hill, Anna Rose and Nick in the late stages.
- Theme of changing social position, craft, and research.

**Classification: E7 historical/literary interpretation with contradicted E6 lineage assertions.** The bloodline statement conflicts with `family/README.md`: Saint Kilian's name is a devotional and onomastic connection, not verified descent. “Clegwallabog” is already documented in `family/maternal-killion-line.md` as a fabricated genealogy record. Specific ship/passenger identities for the famine claim are not demonstrated in this text. Treat this as a preserved earlier narrative, not genealogical authority.
