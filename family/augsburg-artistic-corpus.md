# The Augsburg artistic corpus

The documented core, with repositories and accession numbers. Designed to expand
into a catalogue raisonné.

This is **reception history and craft history**, not part of the medieval cult.
Its value is independent: it is the best-evidenced thing in the whole family
archive.

---

## Lucas Kilian (1579–1637)

Painter, draughtsman, engraver. Trained in the Dominicus Custos publishing orbit;
in Italy 1601–04 (Venice, Mantua, Milan), which brought a new ornamental dynamism
into German engraving.

Biography: British Museum **BIOG33770**.

His *Thesenblatt* — the large-format thesis broadsheet commissioned by Catholic
universities to announce academic defences — is his most distinctive contribution:
allegorical landscapes built to be decoded.

The anatomical broadsheets, the Luther broadside and the Counter-Reformation
St Michael in one surviving corpus show something precise about Augsburg: a single
technically sophisticated workshop tradition could carry saint imagery, doctrine,
portraiture, anatomy, city views and print commerce across the confessional
divide.

## Bartholomäus Kilian II (1630–1696)

Son of Wolfgang. Trained in Paris and Italy. The technical peak of the line;

## The workshop that made all of this possible

Augsburg's copper engraving tradition grew directly out of goldsmith engraving
technique; the city lexicon places the first Augsburg copper engravings around
1570.

**Bartholomäus Kilian I** (1548–1583, *not* 1588) came from Silesia as a
goldsmith, settled at Augsburg around 1575 and attained master status in 1578.
After his death his widow **Maria Pfeiffelmann** married **Dominicus Custos**, a
Netherlandish engraver with a significant Augsburg workshop. That marriage
brought Lucas and Wolfgang under one of the most innovative printmakers of the
era.

So the pivot from gold to copper — from unique object to reproducible image — ran
through a second marriage. The transmission was not of blood but of a workshop.

## A note on register

The longer monograph kept at
[augsburg-artistic-corpus-source.txt](augsburg-artistic-corpus-source.txt) reads
this corpus as a continuous "Sacred Craft" running from goldsmithing to recursive
intelligence, and frames the *Thesenblatt* and the Tree of Life as precursors of
the Kouns–Killion Paradigm.

That is **interpretation**, and belongs with the rest of it in
[interpretive/](../interpretive/). The table above is the documentary layer: works,
dates, repositories, accession numbers. The two should not be read as one
register, and the monograph is kept here as written so the difference is visible.
