## Generation 5 — the documentary hinge

Joseph Jost Cuntze is the strongest single anchor anywhere in either line, and
everything about the paternal story turns on him.

| Fact | Record |
|---|---|
| Born **1674 at Niederndorf**, son of Johannes and Anna (Schuster) Cuntze | Historic Germanna |
| Married **1704** to Anna Gertrud Reinschmidt | Historic Germanna |
| **Permission to emigrate granted 31 July 1713** | Historic Germanna |
| **Proved in 1724** that he came in **April 1714** with Katherina his wife, son John and daughter Kathrina — recorded as "Joseph Guntz [Coons]" | Spotsylvania court records, transcribed in the *Virginia Historical Magazine* |
| **Will of 1730, probated 10 February 1731**, Stafford County | Historic Germanna |

Named record series, dated, with a court proof and a probated will. This is what
"documented" means.

## Why iron

Niederndorf had an **ironworks dating to at least 1417**. The district was
economically defined by iron, and the family belonged to the steelsmiths' and
toolmakers' guild.

The 1714 Germanna colonists were **invited expressly to develop Governor
Alexander Spotswood's iron properties** in Virginia, and the Virginia sources
credit them with introducing comparatively modern iron-manufacturing methods to
the colony. The migration was a transfer of skilled metalworking labour, not a
general emigration — which is why the guild membership in generation 4 and the
emigration permission in generation 5 belong to the same fact.

The later movement down the Ohio into the Ashland–Ironton–Huntington corridor
kept the family in iron country: a tri-state industrial region built on river
transport, furnaces and rail.

## The weak stretch

Generations 6–11 are plausible and derivative. The Shenandoah–Greenbrier–Ohio–
Kentucky arc is historically sound as a migration pattern, and Kentucky's rivers
were the migration arteries. But person-by-person proof still requires
county-level deed, probate, church and census work.

**George W. Kouns (generation 11) is the bottleneck.** Same-name confusion is
already visible in public material. Until he is cleanly identified, the
Christian → George → Edgar sequence stays provisional. Work outward from Edgar
(1902–1967) and Donald Ray (1932–2003) using death certificates, obituaries,
census households, probate and cemetery records in Greenup, Boyd, Lawrence and
neighbouring counties.

## Name variants

Cuntze · Kuntze · Kuntz · Kunz · Koontz · Coons · Counts · Coontz · Kouns

All of these must be searched together. The 1724 Virginia court record writes him
as **Guntz** and glosses him as **Coons** in the same entry, which is a useful
reminder that the clerk's ear, not the family, produced most of these forms.
