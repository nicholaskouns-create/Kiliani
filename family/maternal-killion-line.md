## ❌ "St Clegwallabog Killian (1490–1555)" — the verdict is fiction

**No such saint exists in any authoritative source.** Not in the *Bibliotheca
Hagiographica Latina*, not in any martyrology, not in the Bollandist indexes, not
in any Augsburg family reference.

**Where the string actually appears — all [C]:**

- **FamilySearch** makes "Clegwallabog Killian" (1500–1555) the father of a "Hans
  Killian" (c. 1516–1583, Augsburg, m. "Ursula Britzin or Zyx" 1540)
- **Geni** styles him "St. Clegwallabog Killian" and makes Hans the father of both
  "Bartholmeous Killian" **and "Dominicus Custos Killian"** — which is impossible.
  Custos was a Netherlander who became the boys' **stepfather** by marrying
  Bartholomäus's widow.
- **Ancestry** member stories by one contributor are attached to "Clegwallabog,
  Saint Kilian/Killian"
- **Wikidata** on Saint Kilian (Q181738) carries an identifier snippet reading
  "Clegwallabog Kilian (est. 640 – )"

That last item explains the "Saint". **A user tree merged the record of the
seventh-century saint into a placeholder person**, and the sainthood travelled
with the merge. The name is an artefact of a database operation.

**What the name is not:** not Irish, not German, not Latin in form. It matches no
BHL entry or martyrology name.

**What it might be, as hypothesis only:** an invented filler for a missing
generation, or a garble of a Welsh hagiographic name such as Clydog or Cledwyn.

**Disposition:** remove from the lineage. Keep the record of it here, because the
plates are published and anyone who has seen them deserves to find the
correction.

## ❌ "Hans Kilian (1516–1583)" — the conflation is now traceable

**No authoritative Augsburg reference names a Hans as Bartholomäus's father.**

The mechanism: the FamilySearch and Geni "Hans Killian, c. 1516–1582/83,
Augsburg" appears to **fuse two separate facts**:

| Element | Actually belongs to |
|---|---|
| birth c. 1516 | **Hans Kilian the printer** (c. 1515 Neuburg an der Donau – 29 Dec 1595 Passau) |
| death 1583 | **Bartholomäus Kilian I**, per the *ADB*'s death year |

So the "progenitor" is one real man's birth year welded to another real man's
death year.

**And a decisive objection:** Bartholomäus came **from Silesia**. A Silesian
immigrant cannot have an Augsburg-born father in Augsburg.

Hans Kilian the printer is a genuinely interesting figure in his own right —
Ottheinrich's printer, later his secretary, keeper of his Paracelsica; his
drawings survive in Heidelberg, Cod. Pal. germ. 302; see J. Telle, "Kilian,
Ottheinrich und Paracelsus", *Heidelberger Jahrbücher* 18 (1974). **[A]** He is
simply not an ancestor of the engravers.

**Disposition:** remove from the lineage. **Begin the Augsburg line with
Bartholomäus I.**

## ⚠ Bartholomäus I's death year — this archive's earlier correction was wrong

The archive previously stated that the authoritative death year is **1583**, not
the plate's 1588. **That is now reversed.**

| Year | Source | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| **1587/88** | **Neue Deutsche Biographie** (article on Lucas Kilian); *Bosls Bayerische Biographie* | **[B] — better founded** |
| 1588 | CERL Thesaurus ("Kilian, Bartholomäus, 1548-1588"); Web Gallery of Art | **[A]/[C]** |
| 1583 | *Augsburger Stadtlexikon*; *ADB* (1882) | older |

The NDB reading is preferable: it is more recent, and it **ties the death to a
dated event** — Dominicus Custos's **1588 marriage** to the widow, **Maria
Pfeyffelmann**, daughter of Christoph Pfeyffelmann and Maria Weyk. Custos is
recorded as stepfather "seit 1588".

**So the plate's 1588 was closer to the truth than the correction this archive
applied to it.** Recorded plainly because an archive that only records other
people's errors is not keeping an honest ledger.

Best practice: cite **d. 1587/88**.

## Reading the gradient

The pattern is worth naming because it is the honest shape of almost every long
family line, and seeing it clearly is more use than wishing it away.

- **Generations 1 and 2 as the plates print them do not exist.** Clegwallabog is
  fiction; Hans is a conflation of two other men. The line starts with
  Bartholomäus I.
- **Bartholomäus I through Philipp are the strongest in the whole archive.**
  Augsburg Stadtlexikon, Neue Deutsche Biographie, British Museum, RKD. Not
  family tradition — catalogued.
- **The 1689–1742 and 1732–1781 generations are the break.** This is where the
  Augsburg record and the American record fail to meet, and everything downstream
  is provisional until it is bridged.
- **The American generations are derivative but coherent**, and the Kentucky end
  is firm again — see [reynolds-hill-branch.md](reynolds-hill-branch.md).

Two solid ends, a gap in the middle, and two phantom generations at the top that
can now be struck. That is a better-defined problem than the archive had before.

## Other Kilians on the plate

Named outside the numbered centre line, and all documented:

- **Lucas Kilian** (1579–1637), brother of Wolfgang — painter, draughtsman,
  engraver; trained in the Custos orbit, seasoned in Italy 1601–04.
- **Bartholomäus Kilian II** (1630–1696), son of Wolfgang.
- **Wolfgang Philipp Kilian** (1654–1732), son of Philipp.
- **Philipp Andreas Kilian** (1714–1759) — in the Deutsche Biographie.
- **Georg Christoph Kilian** (1709–1781) — engraver, collector, and the family's
  own chronicler. His *Kunst und Ehrengedächtnis des Kilian'schen Geschlechts* is
  in the Staats- und Stadtbibliothek Augsburg, and it is the **single most
  important unexamined document for this line**.
