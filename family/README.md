# The family

Two lines, iron and gold. Both real. Neither descends from a saint.

---

## The boundary, stated first

**No descent from Saint Kilian is claimed or claimable.**

No hagiographic source — not the *Passio minor*, not the *Passio maior*, not the
Vatican manuscript, not any papal text — names a wife, a child, a sibling line or
a hereditary estate for Kilian. He is presented throughout as a bishop and
itinerant missionary among clerical companions. The *Passio* records a celibate
cleric.

The shared name is real and it is not nothing. Killena, Chilianus, Kilian,
Killian, Killion is a chain of **onomastic and devotional continuity**: a saint's
name taken up, carried, altered by migration and spelling, and kept. That is a
true thing about this family. It is not descent.

An unbroken maternal line to c. 640 cannot be established from any extant source
before roughly 1200, and nothing here attempts it.

The defensible formulation:

> The later Kilian / Killion family inherited an onomastic and cultural
> association with the cult of Saint Kilian.

The unsupported one:

> The family is genealogically descended from Saint Kilian.

An earlier plate asserted the second, with St Kilian as generation 1 of a
thirteen-generation maternal chain. That assertion is withdrawn. The plate is
kept, dated, in [recensions/](../recensions/), and the withdrawal is recorded in
[CHANGES.md](../recensions/CHANGES.md).

**A modern plate cannot be its own primary evidence.** The earlier lineage
documents cited the plates themselves as the record of the sequence the plates
asserted. That is circular, and naming it here is the point of this directory.

## What the evidence actually supports

Stated plainly, because it is stronger than the version that needed a saint.

**The maternal line is a craft dynasty.** The Augsburg Kilians were goldsmiths
who became one of the best-documented engraving houses of early modern Europe.
Bartholomäus Kilian the Elder, a goldsmith from Silesia, settled at Augsburg;
after his death his widow Maria Pfeiffelmann married the Netherlandish engraver
Dominicus Custos, and the Kilian sons Lucas and Wolfgang trained in that
workshop. The line then runs through a century of portraiture, cartography,
anatomical broadsheets, thesis plates and confessional print. Georg Christoph
Kilian became the family's own chronicler; his manuscript *Kunst und
Ehrengedächtnis des Kilian'schen Geschlechts* survives in the Staats- und
Stadtbibliothek Augsburg. **The family became an archive.** This one did too.

**The paternal line is an ironworking district.** The Cuntze / Kuntze family of
Niederndorf and Oberfischbach in Nassau-Siegen are traceable to around 1500 in a
region economically defined by iron — Niederndorf had an ironworks by at least
1417, and Johannes Kuntze and all his sons belonged to the steelsmiths' and
toolmakers' guild. Joseph Jost Cuntze received permission to emigrate on
31 July 1713 and proved in 1724 that he had arrived in April 1714, with the
Germanna colony recruited for Governor Spotswood's Virginia iron enterprise.

**Gold and iron are therefore not only metaphors.** They name two real regional
craft worlds: Augsburg precious-metal and engraving work, Siegerland ironworking
and toolmaking.

**The convergence is Appalachian, not medieval.** The Killion branch moves into
Tennessee and Kentucky; the Kouns branch through Virginia, Greenbrier, Ohio and
north-eastern Kentucky. The two lines meet in the twentieth century in Kentucky,
in the marriage of **Anna Rose Killion (1931–2015)** and **Donald Ray Kouns
(1932–2003)**, and both charts terminate in the same living person.

The evidentiary density is concentrated in the middle — sixteenth to eighteenth
century craft, print, migration and settlement. The sacred beginning and the
living endpoint belong to the same story, but it is the middle that is proven.

What joins the two lines is not medieval continuity. It is the repeated survival
of technically trained households through confessional upheaval, Atlantic
migration, Appalachian settlement and industrial reorganisation. Skilled hands
carrying technique, memory and names across four centuries.

That is a harder and better claim than descent from a saint, and unlike descent
from a saint, it is true.
