# Internal discrepancies

Places where the documents inside this archive contradict each other. Recorded
rather than reconciled, because picking a winner without a source would be
inventing the answer.

Each needs one record inspected to settle it.

---

## 1. Alexander Killion's wife ⚠

| Source | Name given |
|---|---|
| *From Passio minor Kiliani to the Modern Archive*, genealogy table | **Edna E. Hill** |
| *Iron and Gold*, citing Anna Rose's obituary | **Elizabeth Hill** |

Both agree the surname is Hill. Anna Rose's obituary names her parents as "Alex
and Elizabeth Hill Killion".

**To settle:** Alex J. Killion's death certificate, or his marriage record, or
the census household. One document closes this.

Note that "Edna E." and "Elizabeth" are not plausible transcription variants of
each other, so this is probably two different records or two different women, not
one misreading.

## 6. Bartholomäus Kilian I's death year

| Source | Year |
|---|---|
| Plate and genealogy table | 1588 |
| Augsburg Stadtlexikon / Deutsche Biographie | **1583** |

*Iron and Gold* flags this directly. The authoritative reference works give 1583.

Worth noting: the plate's **1588** is the year his widow Maria Pfeiffelmann
married Dominicus Custos, which may be the source of the error.

## 7. Dating of the Passio maior

| Source | Date |
|---|---|
| *Geschichtsquellen* repertory (current) | **950–970**, possibly Stephen of Novara |
| Ecclesiastical review | "9th–10th century" |
| Some twentieth-century scholarship | later ninth century |

Not an error so much as a field in motion. The current repertory figure is the
one to cite, with the qualification that the attribution is probable rather than
demonstrated.

## 8. BHL number for the maior

| Source | Number |
|---|---|
| Research report, multilingual dossier | **BHL 4661** |
| Ecclesiastical review | BHL 4661–4664 |

The narrower form is what the current repertory uses. The wider range probably
sweeps in related *vitae* and liturgical pieces.

## 9. "Standard critical collation"

The ecclesiastical review and the earlier plates describe **Emmerich (1896)** as
the standard critical collation. He is not. **Levison (1910)** is the critical
edition. Emmerich is a historical-critical monograph that prints the texts.

This one is resolved and is corrected throughout the archive; it is listed here
because the error appears in a document kept in
[scholarship/](../scholarship/) and a reader will meet it there.

## 10. Which name the living endpoint carries

*Iron and Gold* notes that the maternal plate terminates in **Nicholas "Nick"
Kouns** and the paternal plate in **Nicholas Shane Kouns** — the same person,
named differently on the two charts.

Not an error. Worth keeping because it is a small instance of the thing the whole
archive is about: the same person held by two lines, under two versions of a
name.
