# The Reynolds–Hill branch

The Kentucky generations, from the family papers. This is the best-evidenced
stretch of the maternal side and the part where the archive's own records — not
derivative trees — are the source.

It also **resolves the longest-standing discrepancy in this archive**: the name
of Alexander Killion's wife. See below.

Source: the Kouns Family Tree plates, the Kouns–Reynolds–Hill–Killion plate, and
*Our Family Story*, all in [plates/](plates/). Compiled from family papers,
Reynolds of Pollard family history pages, LDS Family Tree, burial notes and
family photographs.

## The Hill generation

Two children:

- **George Thomas Hill** (17 Aug 1878 – 11 Jan 1943), married **Leanna Reynolds**

- **Elizabeth "Lizzie" Hill** (17 Aug 1895 – 20 Dec 1973) — the direct line.

## ⭐ The resolution

**Elizabeth "Lizzie" Hill (1895–1973) married Alexander "Alex" Killion on
8 June 1910.** She is the grandmother.

This settles [discrepancy 1](discrepancies.md), which had stood since the archive
was built. Two documents disagreed on Alex Killion's wife:

| Source | Name |
|---|---|
| *From Passio minor Kiliani to the Modern Archive* | **Edna E. Hill** |
| Anna Rose's obituary, via *Iron and Gold* | **Elizabeth Hill** |

The obituary is right, and the plates confirm it in detail: given name, both
dates, the marriage date, the parents, and the burial place.

**And the source of the error is now visible.** *Edna Alice Reynolds*
(1861–1956) is generation IV above — Eliza's younger sister, and therefore
Lizzie's **great-aunt**, not her mother. One plate in this archive
([kouns-family-tree-two-column.png](plates/kouns-family-tree-two-column.png))
places a box reading "Edna E. Hill / Killin / Killion" between John Patterson
Hill and Alex Killion, in the slot that belongs to Lizzie.

So "Edna E. Hill" is **Edna Alice Reynolds's given name grafted onto Elizabeth
Hill's position**. An aunt's name migrated one generation down and across a
marriage. That is an ordinary and very human transcription slip, and it is now
traceable rather than merely contradictory.

**Remaining check:** Lizzie's death certificate or the Lancaster Cemetery record,
to confirm 17 Aug 1895 – 20 Dec 1973 against a primary record. The 17 August
birthday is shared with her brother George Thomas Hill, which is unusual enough
to be worth verifying rather than assuming.

## Alex Killion's dates — a propagating typo

Four plates in this archive, four readings:

| Plate | Alex Killion |
|---|---|
| [two-column](plates/kouns-family-tree-two-column.png) | 1885–1961 |
| [expanded](plates/kouns-family-tree-expanded.png) | **27 Sept 1848** – 5 Jan 1961 |
| [Reynolds–Hill–Killion](plates/kouns-reynolds-hill-killion-tree.png) | 1884–1961 |
| [Documented Family Tree](plates/documented-family-tree-alternate-paternal.jpg) | 1884–1961 |

**27 Sept 1848 – 5 Jan 1961 would be 112 years.** The 1848 is almost certainly
picked up from **Arthur Killion (1848–1920)**, the generation immediately above,
which sits directly adjacent on the same plate.

**1884** is the better reading: three plates carry it, and it makes him 25 at his
1910 marriage and 76 at death. **1885** is a one-digit variant of the same.

The same plate also gives **George Thomas Hill as 17 Aug 1828 – 11 Jan 1943**,
which would be 115 years. Three other plates give **1878**. Same class of error:
a digit dropped in the hundreds place.

Neither of these is a scholarly problem. They are typesetting slips in a
beautifully made chart, and they are recorded here so that the next person
working from the plates does not inherit them.

## The Cherokee tradition

Two plates record that the **Baker** line married into the Cherokee population
through **Susan Warren Baker**, and state plainly that this is what the family
papers say:

> *Family papers say she was Cherokee.*
> *The Baker line is remembered in family papers as Cherokee heritage.*

The plates are careful here, and the archive keeps that care. This is
**[D] family tradition** — a real thing the family has transmitted about itself,
recorded as transmitted.

Cherokee descent is among the most frequently reported and most frequently
unverifiable claims in Appalachian family history, which is a fact about the
record-keeping rather than about any particular family. If it is to be tested
rather than simply held, the record sets are the **Dawes Rolls (1898–1914)**,
the **Guion Miller Roll (1906–1911)**, the **1835 Henderson Roll**, and the
enrolment records of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians. Absence from those
rolls is not proof of absence of ancestry — many families were never enrolled,
and some deliberately were not — so a negative result would narrow the claim
without closing it.

Susan Warren Baker would stand behind **Francena Baker Reynolds (1834–1899)**,
and that connection is the place to start.

daughters, which means the photograph independently corroborates the sibling
table above.
