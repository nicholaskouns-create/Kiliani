# The surname: Kilian, Killian, Killion

**The name is polygenetic.** It arose independently in at least two places from
two unrelated roots, which means that bearers of it are not one kin group and
were never expected to be.

This is the single most useful finding for the family archive, and it is a
liberating one rather than a deflating one: it explains why a chain of the name
exists across fourteen centuries *without* requiring a chain of bodies.

---

## Irish: Ó Cillín / Ó Cilleáin

**Patrick Woulfe**, *Irish Names and Surnames* (1923) — **[B]**

Derives Killian and Killion from **Ó Cilleáin** (a variant found in Clare and
Galway) and from **Ó Cillín**. *Cillín* he explains as a pet diminutive of
**Ceallach**, which he glosses "war".

Woulfe records Ó Cillín as borne by **several distinct families** — in Clare,
Galway, Mayo, Westmeath, Offaly, Kildare and Down. *Several distinct families*
is the operative phrase and it is Woulfe's own.

**Edward MacLysaght**, *More Irish Families* — **[B]**

Equates **Killeen** and **Killian** under Ó Cillín, a family of the west, and
notes that "some families which crossed the Shannon and settled in Co. Westmeath
use the form Killian".

He treats **Killen** as usually *MacKillen* (Mac Coilín), an Antrim galloglass
name — a separate origin again.

### Two corrections to this archive's earlier material

- **Mac Giolla Chiaráin should be dropped.** It means "devotee of Ciarán" and no
  onomastic source links it to Killian. It had entered the working notes; it
  comes out.
- **"Little church" (cill + -ín) is a folk etymology.** Attractive, and it is
  what most popular sources give, but the scholarly derivation is from the
  personal name Ceallach, not from *cill*.

## German: the saint's name as a baptismal name

German **Kilian** descends from the saint's name used as a given name, which
became common in Franconia precisely *because* of the Würzburg cult. As a
surname it is therefore **patronymic**: it marks a man whose father was called
Kilian.

### On the "parish church" hypothesis

One of this archive's working notes carried the proposition that commoners
adopting surnames in the period 1200–1300 took the name of their parish church,
which would make many German Kilians onomastic rather than consanguineous.

That proposition is traceable to a single amateur genealogy page, where its
author — Richard Killion, on Kevin Killion's site — **describes it himself as
"pure speculation"**. It is reproduced on the webpage screenshot kept at
[plates/kilian-webpage-second-saint.jpg](plates/kilian-webpage-second-saint.jpg),
where the words "This is pure speculation of course" appear in the original.

It should be cited as what it is: an amateur hypothesis, candidly labelled by its
own author. **[D]**

**But the conclusion survives the weakness of that particular argument.** Whether
German Kilians took the name from a church dedication or from a baptismal name,
either mechanism produces **many unrelated families bearing one name**, not one
descent group. The destination is the same; only the route is speculative.

**One correction.** This archive's Irish research note asserts concentrations in
"Roscommon, Westmeath, Longford, Offaly, also Galway and Mayo". The figures
support all of those **except Offaly**, which has no Killion households in
Griffith's. Woulfe lists Offaly for Ó Cillín generally, so the county is not
wrong for the sept — only for this spelling at this date.

**The Rahara figure matters for the family leads.** Three Killion households in
Rahara parish is exactly consistent with the John Killion of Barnacullen lead.
See [irish-leads.md](irish-leads.md).

## Notable bearers

Recorded because the name's reach is part of its history, not because any of
these are kin.

**Germany**

- **Gustav Killian** (1860–1921) — laryngologist; the founder of bronchoscopy
- **Dietrich Kilian** (1928–1984) — musicologist
- **Michael Kilian** (b. 1949) — jurist, constitutional judge of Saxony-Anhalt
- **Hans Kilian** (c. 1515 Neuburg an der Donau – 29 Dec 1595 Passau) — printer
  to Ottheinrich, later his secretary and keeper of his Paracelsica. **Important
  for this archive for a negative reason** — see
  [maternal-killion-line.md](maternal-killion-line.md).

**United States and elsewhere**

- **James R. Killian** (1904–1988) — president of MIT; first Presidential
  Science Advisor
- **Robert K. Killian** (1919–2005) — Connecticut politician
- **Tom Killion** (b. 1957) — Pennsylvania politician
- **Sean Killion** (b. 1967) — swimmer
- **Redley A. Killion** (b. 1951) — Micronesian politician
- **Jake Kilrain**, born **Joseph John Killion** (1859–1937) — bare-knuckle
  boxer; fought John L. Sullivan in the last bare-knuckle heavyweight
  championship, 1889
- **Dorian Killian** — Fenian lawyer in New York, noted by MacLysaght **[B]**
- **Leo Killion** — songwriter, "Hut Sut Song" **[C]**

**Andreas Killian**, a German immigrant, is identified in hobbyist sources as the
ancestor of many United States Killians, particularly in North Carolina. **[C]**
Given the North Carolina leg of this family's own migration, he is worth
investigating — but the identification is derivative and unverified.

## What this means for the family

The name travelled by **baptism, devotion, parish and clerical error** — not by
descent from one man. Killena became Chilianus became Kilian became Killian
became Killion, and the spelling shifted at each border and each clerk's ear.
The 1724 Virginia court record that writes *Joseph Guntz* and glosses him
*Coons* in the same entry is the same phenomenon on the paternal side.

A name carried for fourteen centuries by many unrelated households, one of which
is this one, is a true and interesting thing. It is also the only version of the
story that the sources will bear.
