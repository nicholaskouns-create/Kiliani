# Passio minor — textual notes

Eight notes keyed to [reading-text-la-en.md](reading-text-la-en.md). Four of
them (4, 6, 7, 8) record defects in the archive's Latin that must be repaired
against Levison before this text can be called an edition.

---

### n. 1 — *Scottica tellus*

In early medieval Latin *Scotia* and *Scotti* mean Ireland and the Irish.
"Scottish land" misleads a modern reader and is the single most consequential
mistranslation in the earlier plate. The later *Passio maior* opens the same way
— *Scotorum genere*.

Rendered here: **"the land of the Scots (Ireland)"**.

### n. 2 — the companions

The plate's *Gallone* and *Arnuvale* are **ablatives**. The nominatives are
**Gallo** and **Arnual**. The earlier English, which gave "Gallonus" and
"Arnualdus", inflected the ablative as though it were the dictionary form and is
corrected here.

*Colonatus* also appears as *Colmanus* in the tradition. The companion lists
fluctuate across witnesses, which is evidence of literary development rather
than of a stable personnel register.

The full party — three priests, one deacon, seven others, and Kilian — numbers
twelve. Public devotion later narrows to the triad Kilian (bishop), Colonatus
(priest), Totnan (deacon).

### n. 3 — *in Australium partem*

*Austria* / *Australia* in Frankish sources means **Austrasia**, the eastern
Frankish kingdom. It is not the adjective *australis*, "southern". Würzburg lies
in eastern Francia, not south of anywhere relevant.

The earlier plate's "to the southern region" is wrong and is corrected here.

### n. 4 — lacuna before the Roman journey ⚠

The plate moves straight from arrival at Würzburg to *illic venientes … Johannem
non invenientes*. The sentence in which Kilian resolves to seek papal
authorisation at Rome **is missing**. As printed, the text implies they looked
for the pope at Würzburg.

The missing sentence must be supplied from Levison. It is deliberately **not
reconstructed here**.

### n. 5 — John and Conon

John is **Pope John V**, died 686. **Conon** reigned 686–687.

This is the chronological peg from which the conventional dates (arrival 686,
death 8 July 689) are derived. It is also the weakest major element in the
narrative: the *Neue Deutsche Biographie* treats the Roman journey as
historically implausible and probably a backward projection of Bonifatian
ecclesiology, in which no mission proceeds without *licentia* from Rome. Remove
the Conon episode and 686 and 689 cease to be independently datable.

The datum is retained in the text without harmonisation, as critical method
requires.

### n. 6 — *Cui dux promittens* ⚠

The clause has **no finite verb**. Either a verb has dropped out (e.g.
*respondit*) or the participle stands loosely for a finite verb. The gap is
marked ⟨…⟩, not filled. Check Levison.

### n. 7 — ⟨dum⟩ *dux* ⚠

The plate reads *et, dux in venatione abesset*, which lacks a conjunction.
**⟨dum⟩** (or *cum*) is supplied. Check Levison.

### n. 8 — *Pippini regis* ⚠

This is the crux of the dating question.

Levison's date of c. 840 rests on the text calling Pippin **the first king of
the East Franks** — a title anachronistic for 752, since the East Frankish
kingdom dates from Louis the German (833). If the plate's bare *Pippini regis*
abridges a fuller title, **the abridgement has removed the key dating
evidence**. The full wording must be restored from Levison.

This note is the reason the archive cannot currently adjudicate its own dating
question. See [README.md](README.md).

## Philological observations

*vitae venerabilis* — conventional hagiographic character formula.

*de magno genere* — social nobility, a standard device establishing that
voluntary exile meant abandoning real status.

*pontificale didicit regere culmen* — *culmen* is office or dignity, not
altitude. "He learned to rule the pontifical height" is mechanically literal;
the functional sense is that sacred education qualified him for episcopal rank.

*Killena → Chilianus* — probably variant linguistic forms rather than a
ceremonial renaming. Manuscript variants: Killena, Kyllena, Chilianus, Kilianus,
Kylianus.

*sacras … litteras* — Christian letters, not classical literature.

*quicquid ad animarum lucrum, quicquid ad propagationem fidei* — the double
missionary motive characteristic of early medieval missionary hagiography.

*nudos sequi Christum* — technical apostolic poverty. *Nudus* = stripped of
possessions.

*firmiter coadunati, relictis omnibus, de propria perrexerunt patria* —
inchoative perfect.

*corrupta suis illecebris* — "corrupted by her own enticements". Moralised and
gendered; part of the text's biblical typology of the hostile royal woman
(Jezebel, Herodias), not neutral judicial evidence.

*in stabulo equorum … cum vasis sacris et vestimentis et scripturis sanctis* —
the assassins attempt to erase not merely bodies but a liturgical community. The
buried sacred objects are also the *inventio* motif: concealed martyrdom with
its apparatus, awaiting discovery.

*sanitatibus* — healings.
