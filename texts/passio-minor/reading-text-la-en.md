# Passio minor Kiliani — reading text with facing translation

**BHL 4660** · anonymous · Latin with English translation

> **Status.** This is an *abridged reading text* with a facing translation. It is
> **not a critical edition**. For citation use Levison's edition in *MGH
> Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum* 5 (Hannover, 1910). The Latin descends from
> the earlier source plate in this archive and **has not yet been collated
> against Levison**. Where the plate's Latin is defective the problem is marked
> and nothing is supplied from memory.

**Conventions.** ⟨angle brackets⟩ enclose editorial supplements. […] marks a
lacuna in the plate. *Italics* in the English mark words with no Latin
equivalent. Numbered notes refer to [apparatus.md](apparatus.md).

The seven section titles are **not in the manuscripts**. They are an editorial
hermeneutic: origin, call and exodus, Roman authorisation, proclamation and
conversion, prophetic confrontation, martyrial witness, glorification.

## I. Incipit

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine, quem Scottica tellus de magno edidit genere. Qui etiam a puerili aetate magnum habuit studium sacras discere litteras et in eis tam perfecte proficiens, ut exinde pontificale didicit regere culmen. Et tunc iam alio vocabatur nomine Chilianus, habens gratiam ad clerum et ad omnem populum. Qui etiam in tantum amore christianae religionis impletus, ut quicquid ad animarum lucrum, quicquid ad propagationem fidei pertinere cognoscebat, devota mente et pio operari studio non cessabat. | There was a man of venerable life named Killena, whom the land of the Scots (Ireland) brought forth from a great family (n. 1). From boyhood he had a great zeal to learn the sacred writings, and he advanced in them so thoroughly that he came in time to govern as a bishop. By then he was called by another name, Chilianus, and was held in favour by the clergy and all the people. He was so filled with love of the Christian religion that whatever he knew to serve the winning of souls or the spread of the faith, he never ceased to pursue with a devoted mind and pious effort. |

## II. Vocatio et discessus — calling and departure

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Contigit autem, ut quadam die evangelica simul et voce dominica ammonitus esset, ubi dicitur (Luc. IX, 23): "Qui vult venire post me, abneget semet ipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me." Haec secum beatissimus vir toto cordis ac mentis affectu pertractans, congregatis in unum suis consociis atque discipulis, id est Colonato ac Gallone et Arnuvale presbyteris et Totnano diacono, aliis septem illis adiunctis, coepit eos persuadere: propria contemni patriamque et parentes secundum Domini evangelium relinquere et nudos sequi Christum. Illi namque statim sacrae admonitioni beati viri obtemperantes firmiter coadunati, relictis omnibus, de propria perrexerunt patria et venerunt in Australium partem ad castellum, quod nominatur Wirziburg. | One day he was admonished by the voice of the Gospel and of the Lord, where it says (Luke 9:23): "Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me." Turning this over with all the affection of his heart and mind, the most blessed man gathered his companions and disciples, namely the priests Colonatus, Gallo and Arnual and the deacon Totnan, with seven others joined to them (n. 2). He began to urge them to despise their possessions, to leave homeland and parents according to the Lord's Gospel, and to follow Christ naked. At once they obeyed the holy man's admonition and, firmly united, left everything, set out from their homeland, and came into Austrasia, to the fortress called Würzburg (n. 3). |

## III. Iter Romanum — the journey to Rome

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| […] Sed illic venientes et apostolicum virum Johannem non invenientes, quoniam iam defunctus est, a sancto papa Conone praedictus Dei pontifex Killena amabiliter et honorifice susceptus est et, quod ad praedicandum verbum Dei petierat, cum omni benignitate consecutus est. | […] (n. 4) But when they arrived there *(in Rome)* and did not find the apostolic man John, since he had already died, the aforesaid bishop of God, Killena, was received kindly and with honour by the holy Pope Conon, and with all goodwill obtained what he had asked, permission to preach the word of God (n. 5). |

## IV. Missio et conversio — mission and conversion

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Revertens igitur cum suis sociis ad praedictum castellum Wirziburg, coepit ibi verbum Dei fiducialiter praedicare. Audiens autem dux Gozbertus verbum praedicationis eius, cum omni populo suo credidit et baptizatus est. | Returning with his companions to the fortress of Würzburg, he began to preach the word of God there with confidence. Duke Gozbert, hearing his preaching, believed together with all his people and was baptised. |

## V. Conflictus de matrimonio — the marriage dispute

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Non multo post tempore persuadebat illum devotus Domini pontifex Kilianus ut dimitteret uxorem suam Geilanam, quae fuerat uxor fratris eius, quia hoc christianae religioni contrarium esse cognoscebat. Cui dux promittens se facturum ⟨…⟩ quod ei praeciperet. | Not long afterward Kilian, the Lord's devoted bishop, urged the duke to put away his wife Geilana, who had been his brother's wife, because he knew this to be contrary to the Christian religion. The duke promised him that he would do whatever he commanded (n. 6). |

## VI. Martyrium — the martyrdom

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Geilana autem, corrupta suis illecebris, cum audisset haec, irata est valde et, ⟨dum⟩ dux in venatione abesset, misit servos suos qui beatum Kilianum cum sociis suis Colonato presbytero et Totnano diacono in stabulo equorum occiderent et corpora eorum ibi sepelirent cum vasis sacris et vestimentis et scripturis sanctis. | But Geilana, corrupted by her own enticements, was greatly angered when she heard this. While the duke was away hunting (n. 7), she sent her servants to kill blessed Kilian and his companions, the priest Colonatus and the deacon Totnan, in the horse stable, and to bury their bodies there with the sacred vessels, the vestments, and the holy books. |

## VII. Translatio — the translation of the relics

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Postea vero, cum Burchardus episcopus Wirziburgensis, praecepto et consilio Zachariae papae et Pippini regis, corpora sanctorum martyrum cum honore transtulisset et in ecclesia beatae Mariae in monte collocasset, miraculis et sanitatibus claruerunt. | Afterward, when Burchard, bishop of Würzburg, by the command and counsel of Pope Zacharias and King Pippin (n. 8), had translated the bodies of the holy martyrs with honour and placed them in the church of blessed Mary on the hill, they became renowned for miracles and healings. |

---

The text as printed here ends with the translation. The *minor* as transmitted
continues a little further; the repertory gives its closing formula as a passage
in which the saints are honourably raised from the tomb under Pippin, ending
*Amen*. That ending is **not** reproduced here because it has not been collated.
See [verification-queue.md](verification-queue.md).

## Biblical and typological register

| Section | Model |
|---|---|
| Vocatio | Luke 9:23; Abrahamic departure (Gen 12:1) |
| Iter Romanum | Petrine authorisation; the *licentia* of the Bonifatian reform |
| Missio | Acts-style mass conversion |
| Conflictus | Lev 18:16 and 20:21; John the Baptist and Herodias |
| Martyrium | the protomartyr Stephen |

*Nudos sequi Christum* is the ascetic formula for dispossession, not literal
nakedness — the fuller form elsewhere in the tradition is *nudos sequi Christum
nudum*. It belongs to the Irish *peregrinatio pro Christo* of the Columbanian
pattern.
