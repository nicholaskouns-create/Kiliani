## The text

Emmerich carries the closing chapter across the page break. Page 9 ends mid-clause
and page 10 completes it:

> p. 9 — *…statim sanitatem receperunt.* **Inlustrante autem⁽³⁹⁾ Domino merita
> eorum cum consilio**
>
> p. 10 — **et praecepto⁽¹⁾ Zachariae papae, mediante Bonifacio⁽²⁾ archiepiscopo,
> a Burchardo primo Wirciburgensium episcopo⁽³⁾ de tumulo honorifice sublati
> sunt, regnante Pippino primo Orientalium Francorum rege feliciter.⁽⁴⁾**

"The Lord making their merits shine forth, by the counsel and command of Pope
Zacharias, through the mediation of Archbishop Boniface, they were honourably
raised from the tomb by Burchard, first bishop of the people of Würzburg, in the
happy reign of Pippin, first king of the East Franks."

## What this supplies

The archive records four elements as missing from its own closing sentence.
All four are present here, in a printed critical text, with an apparatus naming
which manuscripts omit them.

| element the archive lacks | present in Emmerich | witness note |
|---|---|---|
| *mediante Bonifatio archiepiscopo* | **yes** — *mediante Bonifacio archiepiscopo* | W spells *Bonifatio* |
| *primo* Wirziburegensium episcopo | **yes** — *primo Wirciburgensium episcopo* | **M² and Ad omit the whole phrase** |
| *de tumulo honorifice sublevati sunt* | **yes** — *de tumulo honorifice sublati sunt* | see the variant below |
| *Pippino primo orientalium Francorum rege* | **yes** — *regnante Pippino primo Orientalium Francorum rege* | no variant recorded |

The royal formula is the one Levison's c. 840 dating rests on, and it is here
undivided. The *Geschichtsquellen* range and Bigelmair's c. 752 can now be
weighed against the wording rather than against its absence.

**The doxology is a witness-level variant.** The queue asks for "the *Amen*
doxology". Emmerich's text ends at *feliciter* with no doxology, and records one
only for **M²**, abbreviated as *Per Domin. etc.* — the liturgical close written
out by a single twelfth-century witness. On Emmerich's evidence the doxology is
not part of the text; it is a lectionary habit. Levison may record it differently.

## Differences from the fragment the archive already has

These are not trivial and are listed rather than smoothed:

| archive's secondhand fragment | Emmerich p. 10 |
|---|---|
| *sublevati sunt* | ***sublati sunt*** |
| *Bonifatio* | ***Bonifacio*** |
| *Wirziburegensium* | ***Wirciburgensium*** (Sg *uuirziburgensium*, V *Wirciburchgensium*) |
| ends at *sublevati sunt…* | continues to *regnante Pippino … feliciter* |

*Sublati* and *sublevati* are different verbs — *tollo* and *sublevo* — and the
spread of the Würzburg spelling across two editions and three manuscripts is its
own small study.

## What this is not

**It is not Levison.** Emmerich (1896) is an earlier and independent edition of
the same work; Levison, *MGH SS rer. Merov.* 5 (1910), p. 728 remains the
standard, and the queue item asks for Levison specifically. Nothing here should
be entered as a Levison reading.

**It is not a fresh collation.** The witness attributions are Emmerich's, made
from seven manuscripts in the 1890s. No manuscript has been consulted for this
file.

**It does not settle the date.** It restores the evidence the archive's own text
had lost. Whether *primus orientalium Francorum rex* dates the work to c. 840, or
is a later scribal updating of an earlier text, is the argument it makes possible,
not an argument it decides.

## What it does change

Item 1 of the verification queue is marked ⚠ *blocks everything*, on the grounds
that the full wording is in hand only at second hand. It is now in hand from a
printed edition, with an apparatus. The item should be rewritten rather than
ticked: **check Levison for his own readings and for the doxology**, no longer
*recover the sentence*.
