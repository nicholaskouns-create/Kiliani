# Passio minor — printed editions and translations

**Cite the *Passio minor* from Levison.** Nothing in this repository replaces it.

---

## Standard critical edition

Wilhelm Levison, ed., *Passio Kiliani martyris Wirziburgensis*, in *Monumenta
Germaniae Historica, Scriptores rerum Merovingicarum* 5 (Hannover, 1910).

### ✅ The page range is resolved — both figures were right

| Cite | For |
|---|---|
| **711–728** | the **whole section**, including Levison's introduction |
| **722–728** | the **edited Latin text** itself |

Levison's introduction starts at **p. 711**. He discusses the *minor*'s
manuscripts at **717–719** and the *maior*'s at **720**. The edited text runs
**722–728**. The next item in the volume, the *Vitae Audomari, Bertini,
Winnoci*, begins at **p. 729**, which closes the range. **[A, dMGH table of
contents]**

So the two figures circulating in this archive were not a contradiction. They
describe different things, and the archive was citing each correctly for a
different purpose without saying which. Both are now labelled.

Available in the **dMGH viewer** and as an **openMGH TEI download** of SS rer.
Merov. 5.

## Emmerich, 1896 — now confirmed

Franz Emmerich, *Der heilige Kilian, Regionarbischof und Märtyrer*
(Würzburg, 1896).

### ✅ pp. 3–10 is confirmed

The *Passio minor* is printed at **pp. 3–10**. Emmerich discusses the
manuscripts at **p. 2**. The *Passio maior* is at **pp. 11–25**. **[A, via the
Geschichtsquellen edition list]**

The page range that propagated through three documents in this archive while
marked "unconfirmed" turns out to have been correct all along.

Note also the title: the archive has carried *Martyrer*; the repertory gives
**Märtyrer**.

Two corrections that stand:

- Emmerich's 1896 book was a **historical-critical monograph, not a
  dissertation**. His honorary theological doctorate the same year was not earned
  through the Kilian study.
- Emmerich is **not** "the standard critical collation", as this archive's
  earlier material called him. **Levison is.** Emmerich prints the texts.

| The repertory bracket | **750–850** | *Geschichtsquellen* |

The *Index scriptorum novus Mediae Latinitatis* dates it **"c. 840"** and cites
**"BHL 4660-4660a"** — indicating a **sub-variant 4660a** not previously recorded
in this archive.

The repertory also notes that the *minor* **may be modelled on Arbeo of
Freising's *Vita Corbiniani***, which is a lead worth following: a textual
dependence would constrain the date far better than the royal title does.

## Note on what this archive's text is

The Latin in [reading-text-la-en.md](reading-text-la-en.md) descends from the
earlier source plate, which described itself as "reconstructed from Emmerich and
earliest MSS". It is therefore at best a **reading text derived from a
nineteenth-century printing**, with four known defects marked in the
[apparatus](apparatus.md). It is not a transcription of any manuscript and not a
reproduction of Levison.
