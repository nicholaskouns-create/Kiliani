# A modern conflated recension of the Kilian passion

> ## ⚠ This is not the medieval *Passio maior*
>
> *Recensio moderna ex Passione minore et maiore conflata; non editio codicum
> mediaevalium.*
>
> **A modern Latin hagiographic adaptation based on both *Passiones*.** It runs
> the authentic *maior* prologue (*Sanctorum martyrum certamina*) straight into
> the *minor*'s incipit (*Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine*), where the
> authentic *maior* reads *Beatus Kylianus, Scotorum genere nobilibus ortus
> parentibus*. See [CONFLATION-NOTICE.md](CONFLATION-NOTICE.md).
>
> Do not cite this as a tenth-century source. Earlier copies carried a footer
> reading "10th Century • Attributed to Stephan of Novara"; that footer is the
> error this notice replaces.

Retained here as a devotional and literary reconstruction, which is what it is
and what it is good at. Material genuinely characteristic of the *maior* is
marked **[maior]**; material carried over from the *minor* is marked
**[minor]**; modern composition is marked **[modern]**.

---

## Prologus **[maior — authentic opening formula]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Sanctorum martyrum certamina omnibus fidelibus nota sunt, per quae Ecclesia aedificata est et nomen Christi usque ad fines terrae glorificatum. Inter hos gloriosos athletas Christi stat beatus Kylianus, episcopus et martyr Herbipolensis, cuius vitam et passionem nunc plenius pro aedificatione fidelium et firmamento Ecclesiae in his regionibus proponimus. | The struggles of the holy martyrs are known to all the faithful, for through them the Church has been built up and the name of Christ glorified even unto the ends of the earth. Among these glorious athletes of Christ stands the blessed Kilian, bishop and martyr of Würzburg, whose life and passion we now set forth more fully for the edification of the faithful and the strengthening of the Church in these lands. |

An expanded rhetorical prologue is unique to the *maior*; the *minor* has no
prologue at all.

**Here the conflation occurs.** The authentic *maior* continues *Beatus Kylianus,
Scotorum genere nobilibus ortus parentibus*. What follows instead is the *minor*.

## De origine et prima aetate beati Kyliani **[minor, with modern expansion]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine, quem Scottica tellus de nobili genere edidit. A puerili aetate magnum studium sacras discere litteras habuit et in eis tam perfecte proficiens, ut exinde pontificale culmen regere dignus haberetur. Et tunc iam alio nomine Chilianus vel Kylianus vocabatur, gratiam ad clerum et ad omnem populum habens. Tanto amore christianae religionis impletus erat, ut quicquid ad animarum lucrum vel fidei propagationem pertinere cognoscebat, devota mente et pio studio non cessaret operari. | There was a man of venerable life named Killena, whom the land of the Scots brought forth from a noble family. From boyhood he had great zeal to learn the sacred letters. He advanced so thoroughly in them that he was deemed worthy to govern as a bishop. He was also called by another name, Chilianus or Kilianus, and was granted favour both toward the clergy and toward all the people. So filled was he with love of the Christian religion that whatever pertained to the winning of souls or the spread of the faith, he pursued with devoted mind and pious effort, never ceasing. |

*de nobili genere* for the *minor*'s *de magno genere*, and *dignus haberetur*
for *didicit regere*, are the modern hand smoothing the Latin.

## De vocatione et discessu **[minor]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Contigit autem quadam die, ut evangelica simul et dominica voce admonitus esset, ubi dicitur: "Qui vult venire post me, abneget semetipsum et tollat crucem suam et sequatur me." Haec secum beatissimus vir toto cordis ac mentis affectu pertractans, congregatis in unum suis consociis atque discipulis — id est Colonatus ac Gallonus presbyteris, Totnano diacono aliisque septem adiunctis — coepit eos persuadere propria contemnere, patriam et parentes secundum Domini evangelium relinquere et nudos sequi Christum. Illi namque statim sacrae admonitioni obtemperantes firmiter coadunati, relictis omnibus, de propria perrexerunt patria et venerunt in Australium partem ad castellum, quod nominatur Wirziburg. | It happened on a certain day that he was admonished by the voice of the Gospel and of the Lord, where it is written: "Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me." Pondering these words with all the affection of his heart and mind, the blessed man gathered his companions and disciples — Colonatus and Gallo the priests, Totnan the deacon, and seven others joined to them. He began to urge them to despise their possessions, to leave homeland and parents according to the Gospel of the Lord, and to follow Christ naked. At once they obeyed the holy admonition and, firmly united, left everything and set out from their homeland. They came into Austrasia, to the fortress called Würzburg. |

Note that *Arnual* has dropped out of the companion list here, leaving only
two priests where the *minor* has three.

The Latin here reads *Colonatus ac Gallonus presbyteris* — nominative forms
governed by an ablative. The *minor*'s *Colonato ac Gallone et Arnuvale
presbyteris* is correct. This is the modern composer's own reading and is left
exactly as written; it is not the archive's translation error, and it is a
further sign that the text is a modern assembly rather than a witness.

## De itinere Romano **[minor]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Sed illic venientes et apostolicum virum Iohannem non invenientes, quoniam iam defunctus erat, a sancto papa Conone praedictus Dei pontifex Killena amabiliter et honorifice susceptus est et, quod ad praedicandum verbum Dei petierat, cum omni benignitate consecutus est. | But when they arrived there and did not find the apostolic man John, since he had already died, the aforesaid bishop of God, Killena, was received kindly and with honour by the holy Pope Conon, and with all goodwill obtained what he had asked for the preaching of the word of God. |

The papal sequence — the death of John, then Conon — is preserved from the
*minor* unchanged. The lacuna marked at n. 4 of the *minor* is present here too,
and unmarked.

## De missione et conversione Gozberti **[minor + modern expansion]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Revertens igitur cum suis sociis ad praedictum castellum Wirziburg, coepit ibi verbum Dei fiducialiter et *apostolica libertate* praedicare. Audiens autem dux Gozbertus verbum praedicationis eius, cum omni populo suo credidit et baptizatus est, *multis aliis ad Ecclesiam aggregatis. Lux evangelii in illo loco clare refulsit.* | Returning with his companions to the fortress of Würzburg, he began to preach the word of God there confidently and *with apostolic freedom*. Duke Gozbert, hearing his preaching, believed together with all his people and was baptised, *and many others were added to the Church. The light of the Gospel shone brightly in that place.* |

The italicised phrases have no counterpart in the *minor*.

## De conflictu cum Geilana et martyrio **[minor + modern expansion]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Non multo post tempore devotus Domini pontifex Kylianus persuasit duci, ut uxorem suam Geilanam dimitteret, quae fratris eius uxor fuerat, quia hoc christianae religioni contrarium esse cognoscebat. Dux, *verbis sancti viri motus*, se facturum quod ei praeceptum erat promisit. | Not long after, Kilian, the Lord's devoted bishop, urged the duke to put away his wife Geilana, who had been his brother's wife, because he knew this union to be contrary to the Christian religion. The duke, *moved by the holy man's words*, promised to do what was commanded him. |
| Geilana autem, suis illecebris corrupta *et ira inflammata*, cum haec audisset, duce in venatione absente, servos suos misit *ad scelus perpetrandum*. Beatum Kylianum cum sociis suis, presbytero Colonatus et diacono Totnano, in stabulo equorum *comprehenderunt*, *gladio interfecerunt* et corpora eorum ibidem cum vasis sacris, vestimentis et scripturis sanctis sepelierunt, *putantes crimen abscondere et sanctorum memoriam extinguere*. | But Geilana, corrupted by her own enticements *and inflamed with rage*, when she heard this, while the duke was away hunting, sent her servants *to carry out a wicked deed*. They *seized* blessed Kilian together with his companions, the priest Colonatus and the deacon Totnan, in the horse stable, *killed them with the sword*, and buried their bodies in that same place together with the sacred vessels, the vestments and the holy writings, *thinking thereby to hide their crime and extinguish the memory of the saints*. |

Note that the duke's promise here has a finite verb (*promisit*), where the
*minor*'s *Cui dux promittens* does not. The modern hand has repaired the defect
silently — which is exactly why the *minor*'s version must be collated against
Levison rather than against this text.

## De miraculis post martyrium et historia Atalongi **[maior]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Verumtamen sanguis martyrum semen est Ecclesiae, nec Dominus famulos suos diu absconditos esse permittit. Signa divina et miracula mox in loco sepulturae apparere coeperunt. Accidit autem ut quidam presbyter nomine Atalongus, pueros litteris erudiens, quadam die iuniores pueros, divino spiritu impulsos, magna voce clamantes audiret: "Kylianus signa facit! De loco, in quo indigne iacet, tolli debet!" Atalongus vero vocem et visionem neglexit. Ob hoc caecitate percussus est in poenam infidelitatis et temeritatis suae. | Yet the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church, and the Lord does not permit His servants to remain long hidden. Divine signs and miracles soon began to appear at the place of their burial. It happened that a certain priest named Atalongus, who was instructing boys in letters, one day heard the younger children, divinely moved, cry out with a loud voice: "Kilian is working miracles! He must be taken up from the place where he lies unworthily!" But Atalongus disregarded both the voice and the vision. For this he was struck with blindness, in punishment for his unbelief and rashness. |
| Postea, a quodam rustico de loco certior factus, ad sepulchrum martyrum ductus est. Ibi ferventer oravit, temeritatem suam agnoscens et intercessionem beati Kyliani pro restitutione visus implorans. Dominus, misericordia motus, humilis orationem exaudivit. Atalongus visum recepit et, gaudio repletus, miraculum publice praedicavit. Fama martyrum per regionem celeriter divulgata est. | Later, informed of the place by a certain rustic, he was led to the tomb of the martyrs. There he prayed fervently, acknowledging his rashness and begging the intercession of blessed Kilian for the restoration of his sight. The Lord, moved by mercy, heard the prayer of the humble man. Atalongus received his sight again and, filled with joy, proclaimed the miracle publicly. The fame of the martyrs spread swiftly through the region. |

This is the *inventio* mechanism: the concealed grave becomes a public shrine
through children, a sceptic's blindness, a rustic guide and a healing. The
*minor* has none of it.

## De relatione ad Bonifatium et episcopatus institutione **[maior]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Haec fama ad aures venerabilis archiepiscopi Bonifatii pervenit. Ex eo tempore investigavit archipraesul venerabilissimus, qualiter oppidum, ubi sanctorum corpora posita erant, episcopali dignitate sublimaretur, ut martyrum memoria digne honoraretur et Ecclesia in illis partibus firmius stabiliretur. | This report reached the ears of the venerable archbishop Boniface. From that time the most venerable archbishop investigated how the town where the bodies of the saints had been laid might be raised to episcopal dignity, so that the memory of the martyrs might be fittingly honoured and the Church in those parts more firmly established. |

The weld between martyr cult and the founding of the bishopric, and the clearest
statement of why these texts mattered politically.

## De translatione per Burchardum **[minor + maior]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Postea vero, cum Burchardus episcopus Wirziburgensis, praecepto et consilio Zachariae papae et Pippini regis, *mediante sancto archiepiscopo Bonifatio*, corpora sanctorum martyrum cum omni honore *de loco indigno* transtulisset, ea in ecclesia beatae Mariae in monte collocavit. Ibi cum reverentia condita sunt. Ex illo tempore martyres per multa miracula et sanitates claruerunt. *Caeci visum, claudi gressum receperunt, et omnes qui fide ad eorum tumulum accesserunt, remedia quae petierunt obtinuerunt.* | Afterward, when Burchard, bishop of Würzburg, by the command and counsel of Pope Zacharias and King Pippin, *and with the mediation of the holy archbishop Boniface*, had translated the bodies of the holy martyrs with all honour *from their unworthy resting place*, he placed them in the church of blessed Mary on the hill. There they were enshrined with reverence. From that time the martyrs became renowned through many miracles and healings. *The blind received sight, the lame walked, and all who came in faith to their tomb obtained the remedies they sought.* |

Note the bare *Pippini regis* again, with no royal title — the same abridgement
that removed Levison's dating evidence from the *minor*. See
[../passio-minor/apparatus.md](../passio-minor/apparatus.md) n. 8.

## Conclusio **[modern]**

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Ita beatus Kylianus cum sociis suis Colonatus et Totnano, bonum certamen certando et cursum consummando, martyrii coronam accepit. Eorum passio multorum fidem confirmavit, et eorum translatio a primo Herbipolensi episcopo gloriosam fundationem ecclesiae illius in martyrum sanguine stabilivit. Lux quae in Scotia coepit et in Wirziburg refulsit, fideles usque hodie illuminat, ad laudem et gloriam Domini nostri Iesu Christi, qui cum Patre et Spiritu Sancto vivit et regnat, Deus per infinita saecula saeculorum. Amen. | Thus blessed Kilian, with his companions Colonatus and Totnan, having fought the good fight and finished the course, received the crown of martyrdom. Their passion strengthened the faith of many, and their translation by the first bishop of Würzburg established for that church a glorious foundation in the blood of martyrs. The light that began in Ireland and shone at Würzburg illumines the faithful to this day, to the praise and glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns, God through endless ages. Amen. |

A newly composed synthesis. The earlier English rendered *in Scotia* as "in
Scotland"; corrected here to **Ireland**, consistently with
[n. 1](../passio-minor/apparatus.md) of the *minor*.
