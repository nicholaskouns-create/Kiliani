# Conflation notice

**The bilingual *Passio maior* in this archive is a modern composition. It is not
the medieval work, and it must not be cited as one.**

*Recensio moderna ex Passione minore et maiore conflata; non editio codicum
mediaevalium.*

---

## The test that fails

The authentic *Passio maior* has a rhetorical prologue and then begins its
narrative afresh:

> **Prologue:** *Sanctorum martyrum certamina…*
>
> **Narrative incipit:** *Beatus Kylianus, Scotorum genere nobilibus ortus
> parentibus…*

The archive's bilingual text opens with a plausible form of the authentic
prologue — and then continues:

> *Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine…*

That is the incipit of the ***minor***.

A text that runs the *maior*'s prologue straight into the *minor*'s opening is a
conflation of two works. This single transition is the decisive diagnostic for
whether a Kilian text is a medieval witness or a modern assembly, and it is the
test the archive's own *maior* does not pass.

## What else is modern in it

- modern section titles (*De origine et prima aetate*, *De vocatione et
  discessu*, and the rest)
- a smooth, readable English translation of a kind no medieval witness carries
- rhetorical expansions worked into the *minor*'s sentences — *apostolica
  libertate*, *Lux evangelii in illo loco clare refulsit*, *multis aliis ad
  Ecclesiam aggregatis*, *putantes crimen abscondere et sanctorum memoriam
  extinguere*
- marginal annotations in a modern critical voice
- a newly composed concluding synthesis
- the running footer "10th Century • Attributed to Stephan of Novara", which
  presents the whole assembly as a tenth-century source

The **Atalongus episode** and the **Boniface-to-bishopric transition** are
genuinely characteristic of the *maior* and are not inventions of this archive.
Their presence is what makes the text a conflation rather than a fabrication:
real *maior* material has been grafted onto a *minor* spine.

## What remains defensible

The attribution to **Stephen of Novara** is defensible — but only for the
medieval *Passio maior* in its proper textual form, and even there it stays
qualified as *possibly* or *probably*. There is no authorial colophon. The
attribution rests on Petersohn's and Hoffmann's analysis and on Stephen's
presence as a teacher at the Würzburg cathedral school from 952.

The attribution cannot be carried over to this archive's conflated text, because
that text is not the work being attributed.

## What this file is for

Not to discard the document. It is a coherent devotional and literary
reconstruction, and it may be kept and read as one. The notice exists so that the
label travels with the text, in both directions: anyone who finds the bilingual
edition finds this notice, and anyone reading this notice can find the text.

The honest classification is:

> **A modern Latin hagiographic adaptation based on both *Passiones*.**

It belongs to the same class as the archive's earlier *minor* plate: real
material, real effort, wrong label. The label is the only thing that needed
fixing.

## If a text of the medieval maior is wanted

There is no universally adopted modern critical edition based on a complete
stemmatic reassessment of all known witnesses. That is a real gap in the field,
not a gap in this archive. The available routes:

- **Canisius**, *Antiquae lectiones* (1603) — the first complete printing
- ***Acta Sanctorum***, Iulii II (1721)
- **Emmerich** (1896), pp. 11–25 — *confirmed* page range
- **Surius** (1573), incomplete; **Serarius** (1598), a republication
- **Bigelmair**, German translation
