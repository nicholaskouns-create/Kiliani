# Texts

| Work | BHL | Date | Directory |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Passio minor Kiliani* | 4660 | 750–850 *(disputed: c. 752 / c. 788 / c. 840)* | [passio-minor/](passio-minor/) |
| *Passio maior Kiliani* | 4661 | c. 950–970, possibly Stephen of Novara | [passio-maior/](passio-maior/) |

The *minor* is the earlier continuous narrative and the source of the *maior*.
The *maior* circulated far more widely — roughly forty manuscripts against a
handful — so the expanded version is the one the Middle Ages read.

> ⚠ The *maior* text held here is **a modern conflation**, not the medieval work.
> See [passio-maior/CONFLATION-NOTICE.md](passio-maior/CONFLATION-NOTICE.md).

## The decisive diagnostic

The one test that separates a medieval witness from a modern assembly:

| Work | Opens |
|---|---|
| *minor* | *Fuit vir vitae venerabilis Killena nomine…* |
| *maior*, prologue | *Sanctorum martyrum certamina…* |
| *maior*, narrative | *Beatus Kylianus, Scotorum genere nobilibus ortus parentibus…* |

A text that runs the *maior*'s prologue into the *minor*'s incipit is conflated.

## The asymmetry

The stable core across all layers: an outsider or Irish missionary; activity at
Würzburg and in Thuringia; the companions Colonatus and Totnan; violent death
arising from conflict with secular authority; a subsequent cult centred on
Würzburg.

Everything around that core expands with time — names, dates, speeches,
psychology, miracles, punishments. **Stable core, expanding periphery** is the
normal signature of a living hagiographic tradition, and it is the reason the
later and fuller text is the less reliable one.
