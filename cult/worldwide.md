# The cult beyond Würzburg

---

## Canonisation — there was none, and there could not have been

**No medieval papal canonisation act for Kilian exists or is reported.**

His cult is **pre-congregational**. It rests on the episcopal elevation of the
relics (743/44 or 752), which the *Passio* says took place with papal counsel —
counsel being a different thing from an act.

The decisive fact: **formal papal canonisation begins only with Ulrich of
Augsburg in 993.** **[B]** Kilian's cult predates the entire juridical
mechanism by some two and a half centuries.

So any claim that a named pope canonised Kilian on a precise date should be
rejected unless an authentic act, register entry or diplomatic edition is
produced. None has been.

## The feast

**8 July** — Kilian, Colonatus, Totnan.

An **optional memorial in the regional calendar for the German language region**
since **15 November 1972**. **[A, Wikidata statement]**

Patron of the **Diocese of Würzburg**, and also one of the patron saints of the
**Diocese of Erfurt**. **[A]**

The secular **Kiliani-Volksfest** at Würzburg was not verified from a primary
source in the research behind this file.

## Papal references

### Benedict XVI, Erfurt, 24 September 2011 — read carefully

Two different texts circulate and the archive's earlier bibliography conflated
them.

**The official vatican.va text** names Kilian among the Erfurt patrons: **[A]**

> the patron saints of the Diocese of Erfurt: Saint Elizabeth of Thuringia,
> Saint Boniface and Saint Kilian

and later:

> Like Saints Kilian, Boniface, Adelar, Eoban and Elizabeth of Thuringia, we want
> to engage with our fellow citizens…

**The provisional text released through Vatican Radio** adds the fuller sentence:

> Even before the Anglo-Saxon missionaries, Saint Kilian, an itinerant missionary
> from Ireland, was at work in Thuringia. Together with two companions he died in
> Würzburg as a martyr…

**This archive previously attributed that fuller sentence to the official text in
seven languages.** It belongs to the **provisional** text. The correction matters
because the fuller sentence is the one that carries the substantive claims —
Irish origin, Thuringia, two companions, martyrdom at Würzburg — and those should
be cited to Vatican Radio's provisional release, not to the final homily.

Either way, the homily **receives** the devotional tradition. It pronounces on
nothing: not the dating of the *Passiones*, not the Roman journey, not the year
689, not Geilana's guilt.

## Ireland

### Mullagh, County Cavan

The claim that Kilian was **born at Mullagh** is **local tradition**. **[D]**

The *Passio* says only *Scottica tellus* — "the land of the Scots". It names no
place in Ireland at all.

Further claims from St Kilian's Heritage Centre with **no early basis**:

- education at **Rosscarbery** and **Tuosist**
- a monastic stay on **Iona** — this one traces to **Trithemius**, whose
  historical writing is unreliable. See
  [../saints/cilline-of-iona.md](../saints/cilline-of-iona.md).

### St Kilian's Heritage Centre, Mullagh

- Officially opened by **President Mary Robinson** in **1995**. *The Anglo-Celt*
  (4 February 2010) gives 1995; raisedbogs.ie gives **7 May 1995**, with work
  begun in 1993.
- Funders listed by *The Anglo-Celt*: the **Diocese of Würzburg**, the
  International Fund for Ireland, Cavan Monaghan Rural Development Co-op, FÁS,
  Bord Fáilte, Wellman International, Cavan County Enterprise Board, and the
  Mullagh community. **[C/A]**
- askaboutireland's figure of **one-third German funding is not corroborated**.

Local sites also include **St Kilian's Church**, **Teampall Cheallaigh**, and a
**Kilian's well at Cloughbally**.

### The relics in Cavan, 2024

**The relics travelled from Würzburg to Cavan, 2–6 October 2024**, in a
**Kilmore–Würzburg diocesan partnership**. **[A, Irish Catholic Bishops'
Conference]**

Thirteen centuries after an Irishman is said to have left for Franconia, the
bones came back for five days. Whatever the *Passio* can and cannot prove, that
happened, and it is recent enough to be documented properly.

## Dedications

### Germany

- **Würzburg Cathedral** — the *Kiliansdom*
- **Neumünster**, Würzburg — over the traditional martyrdom site; the crypt holds
  the bones
- **Kilianskirche, Heilbronn** — ⚠ a genuine Kilian dedication, but **Lutheran
  since the Reformation and not a cathedral**. This archive's webpage screenshot
  calls it "another St. Killian Cathedral in Heilbron"; that is wrong on both
  counts.

### United States

- **St. Kilian, Minnesota** (a community named for the saint)
- **St. Kilian Congregation**, Hartford, Wisconsin
- **St. Kilian**, Chicago

### Ireland

- **St Kilian's German School**, Dublin **[C]**
- St Kilian's Church and Teampall Cheallaigh, Mullagh

### Not yet surveyed

**Austrian, British and Australian dedications were not compiled** in the
research pass behind this file. That is an incomplete section, not a negative
finding. Given the German diaspora, Austrian and Australian dedications are
likely to exist.
