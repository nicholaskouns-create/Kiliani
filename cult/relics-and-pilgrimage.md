# Relics, translation and pilgrimage

---

## The translation — three dates, all tradition-dependent

| Date | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **743/44 or 752** | *Geschichtsquellen* repertory **[A]** | The *Passio minor* ends with Burchard's translation |
| **8 July 752** | *Catholic Encyclopedia* and others **[B]** | With Pope Zachary's sanction — the conventional date |
| **788, in Charlemagne's presence** | Würzburg diocesan pilgrimage history **[A]** | A second, later translation |

These are not necessarily in conflict: an elevation under Burchard and a later
translation under Charlemagne can both have happened. But each rests on the
hagiographic and diocesan tradition rather than on a contemporary record, and the
archive should not present any one of them as fixed.

The *Passio minor*'s own closing chapter attributes the elevation to Burchard
"with the counsel and command of Pope Zacharias, through the mediation of
Archbishop Boniface" — see
[../texts/passio-minor/apparatus.md](../texts/passio-minor/apparatus.md) n. 8.

## The pilgrimage, dated

From the diocesan account **[A]**:

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1127 | Bishop Embricho extends the pilgrimage to **eight days** |
| 1401 | **Boniface IX** grants an indulgence |
| 1795 | The relics are **put away** in a chamber of the cathedral |
| 1803 | The feast **lapses** with secularisation |
| **1849** | The relics are **rediscovered** in that chamber |
| 1935 | The present form of the **Kiliani-Wallfahrtswoche** is established |
| 1949 | Bishop **Julius Döpfner** returns the relics from wartime safekeeping at Gerolzhofen to the Neumünster |

Sources for the 1795/1849 sequence: Haus der Bayerischen Geschichte, *Klöster in
Bayern: Neumünster*.

**The gap from 1795 to 1849 is the most important fact in this file.** The cult's
physical continuity was broken for half a century, within living memory of
people's grandparents. What survived the break was the written tradition and the
institution — which is precisely the argument the *Passiones* were written to
make.

## Where the relics are now

Two statements circulate and **both are correct**, because the relics are in two
places:

| Part | Location |
|---|---|
| **The skulls** | set into the **high altar of the Kiliansdom** (Würzburg Cathedral) |
| **The bones** | the **crypt of the Neumünster**, the adjacent church |

German: *"die Schädel sind in den Hauptaltar des Würzburger Kiliansdoms
eingelassen, die Gebeine finden sich in der Krypta der benachbarten
Neumünsterkirche"* (domradio.de).

St Kilian's Heritage Centre adds that the cathedral altar has held the
**three-skull shrine** since its consecration in **1967**.

So where a source says "in the Altar of Würzburg Cathedral" (the Irish bishops)
and another says the Neumünster crypt, neither is wrong. The Neumünster stands
over the traditional site of the martyrdom.
